Core principles for selection for FWO funding
The most important criterion on which all applications submitted to the FWO are assessed is scientific excellence.
In addition, the principle for FWO’s funding of research is that the applicants themselves determine the subject of that research (bottom-up principle). It is only calls within the framework of European Partnerships and other thematic calls that must comply with a specific theme.
A third central principle is that applications are assessed in comparison with other applications from the same universities and research institutes. Thus, FWO is always about inter-institutional competition.
Last but not least there is a guarantee of transparency, which we also expect from researchers and partners, and equal opportunities.
Application programmes
There are application programmes for both basic research (FO) and strategic basic research (SBO).
Individual researchers: fellowship FR and SB fellowship, postdoc FR, mobility
Research teams: projects FR, SBO projects, European projects, International cooperation, brain gain programme Odysseus
Research infrastructure
Scientific prizes
Submitting an application
All applications to the FWO are done completely digital, via our e-loket. You can complete and edit personal details and application forms up to the call deadline.
Assessment of applications
Applications are always assessed by scientific experts who themselves are conducting world-class research in your domain or discipline (peer review). FWO brings these researchers together on its panels and juries. There is a web fact sheet for each application programme indicating which panel is responsible for which disciplines and is therefore suitable for your application.
In addition to these internal assessors, we usually (but not for applicants and applications of the smaller type) call upon external assessors for each specific application on the basis of their expertise on its subject matter.
Panels and other evaluation bodies work under the guidance of the FWO administration and the supervision of the Board of Directors, which sets the rules and procedures and makes the formal decision to award applications.
Panel evaluation sometimes involves an interview (for applicants for pre- and doctoral fellowships selected for this purpose during pre-selection) or an opportunity for interim reaction to evaluators' findings ( rebuttal ) before the integral panel takes a final selection decision (for FO and SBO projects).
You can find out more about the various administrative steps and requirements that follow an award on our dedicated web pages .
Selection or rejection and thereafter
Once the formal decision to award or reject an applicant has been made, the applicant is informed as soon as possible. Upon award, an agreement is made between the FWO and the beneficiary regarding the nature and use of the funding. All applicants receive feedback on the assessment, which may enable them further to improve an awarded application if necessary or to consider whether or not to resubmit a rejected application and how to adapt it for resubmission.

Would you like to stay informed?
The FWO runs an annual call for candidates for its Review College Fundamental Research. The Review College is made up of highly qualified and experienced research experts who may be called upon to join an FWO expert panel, more specifically one of the panels to evaluate fellowships or research projects in fundamental research. The panels meet once or twice a year in Brussels to evaluate research applications from researchers in Flanders. Panels consist of 12 experts who are top researchers in their own discipline(s) and who, from a generalist background, can also evaluate research applications from their broader field. The majority of panel members in each panel are not affiliated with a Flemish research institution; Flemish researchers are, however, certainly also invited to respond to our call Membership of the Review College does not commit you to serving in the panel, but enables FWO to contact you with an invitation to join a panel.
Would you like to stay informed?
Our call opens annually in the second half of March and closes in early May. If you would like to be informed when this call opens again, let us know via communicatie@fwo.be .

I have a travel grant
This webpage aims to bring together the most important practical and administrative information for the benefit of beneficiaries of an FWO travel grant (incoming and outgoing).
Contact
Do you have a question on your travel grant, contact us on interprog@fwo.be

Important!
You can only use your travel grant if your Flemish hosting institution, regardless of the destination, has approved the travel. For this, contact the advisory committee responsible at your host institution, especially for high-risk destinations (see advice FPS Foreign Affairs ). Together with the positive travel advice from your host institution, you should also check whether insurer AIG covers this destination. More info can be found under section 'Travel accident insurance'.
Before departure
FWO fellowship holders are required to complete and submit the 'reporting foreign stay' form online in their e-desk before departure.
Any change to the stay already granted requires prior approval from the FWO (interprog@fwo.be) .
Sustainable travel policy
The FWO is committed to raising awareness around climate change by putting sustainable travel policy at the forefront of its funding channels. The following guideline applies: for all travel of less than six hours – based on the researcher's work address – the researcher is invited to use a technological alternative (e.g. videoconferencing) or using a means of transport other than plane . For sustainable travel within the FWO channels, the FWO provides researchers with a visual route planner when planning their trip, as well as an easy-to-use city map as an aid for estimating travel time. There is more information on the FWO's sustainable travel policy on this page .
Booking travel (air and train tickets) in the context of an FWO travel grant
An FWO travel grant provides for travel costs Brussels - venue congress/residence abroad, there and back. A departure from this rule is possible, but any extra cost thus incurred will have to be borne by the beneficiary. If the extended stay abroad is carried out in multiple locations, only the first trip (outward) and the last trip (return) will be reimbursed separately.
Air tickets (always in economy class) or train tickets (always the cheapest fare) within the framework of an FWO travel credit can only be booked via a travel agency authorised by the FWO (Uniglobe South West Travel and Omnia Travel) . Always state your FWO file number in all correspondence with the travel agent, as well as the following Bluebiz Account number: BE02727. Where necessary, please check whether you in fact will be able to obtain a valid visa for your destination before booking your travel.
Bookings through another travel agency will not be refunded!
Before the granting of a FWO travel grant:
The researcher books their travel with Uniglobe South West Travel or Omnia Travel before FWO allocation has taken place (researcher notifies the travel agent of their FWO application).
Uniglobe South West Travel or Omnia Travel will then make the booking under the FWO client code.
Uniglobe South West Travel or Omnia Travel invoices the researcher directly.
The researcher pays the invoice to Uniglobe South West Travel or Omnia Travel themselves.
If the FWO awards the travel grant, the researcher shows Uniglobe South West Travel or Omnia Travel the award (letter and/or e-mail).
Uniglobe South West Travel or Omnia Travel credits the first invoice and refunds the amount to the researcher.
At the same time, Uniglobe South West Travel or Omnia Travel prepares a new invoice made out to the FWO, indicating the booking number (FWO file number).
The FWO pays the invoice to Uniglobe South West Travel or Omnia Travel (third-party payer system).
After the granting of a FWO travel credit:
The researcher makes the booking with Uniglobe South West Travel or Omnia Travel on the basis of the grant letter and/or e-mail already received regarding the approval of the travel credit.
Uniglobe South West Travel or Omnia Travel will then make the booking under the FWO client code.
Uniglobe South West Travel or Omnia Travel invoices FWO.
The FWO pays the invoice to Uniglobe South West Travel or Omnia Travel (third-party payer system).
Transport costs from your place of residence to an airport/railway station in Belgium will only be reimbursed if public transport was used. Transport costs from an airport/railway station abroad to the location of the conference/accommodation can also be reimbursed. Here, too, the transport should preferably be performed by public transport. For each reimbursement of these specific transport costs the necessary receipts must be sent to FWO.
When travelling by car , a mileage allowance is paid to FWO travel credit beneficiary, with a maximum of €800.00. This mileage allowance can be claimed using this form .
During the stay abroad
A flat-rate daily allowance
Where applicable, the FWO travel grant includes, in addition to reimbursement of travel costs, a lump-sum daily allowance of EUR 66 per day, with a maximum of EUR 1,650 per month:
For a short stay or workshop abroad of eight days or more, the full amount granted is usually deposited into the beneficiary's account ahead of departure.
For a longer stay abroad, 80% of the granted amount is usually deposited into the beneficiary's account ahead departure. The remaining balance is deposited upon receipt of financial justification (see reporting).
These allowances are personal and cannot be transferred or used to cover the travel or subsistence expenses of anyone other than the beneficiary. Only the costs relating to the allocated period can be submitted for reimbursement.
Admissible costs:
Housing
Rent (and related insurance) always substantiated by original proof of payment.
Internet and telephony
Electricity, water and gas
Food
Child-related costs abroad (such as school and childcare)
Public transport locally when commuting, including rental of a bicycle or car where public rental is not available.
Expenses of more than EUR 500 should be accompanied by original supporting documents. For daily recurring expenses, an estimate of the costs is sufficient.
The following cannot be reimbursed from the lump-sum daily allowance: travel costs of family members (e.g. air tickets, local travel, visa costs); leisure costs (e.g. television subscription, sports club membership); medical costs; costs for products with lasting value (e.g. furniture and clothing); and research-linked costs (e.g. reagents, test animals, plants).
Travel insurance
All beneficiaries are automatically covered by travel and assistance insurance with AIG . Please note that any travel to 'dangerous regions and countries at war', as stipulated in the insurance policy between FWO and AIG, are not covered by this policy*. For those destinations, researchers are given the option of taking out individual travel accident insurance with AIG against payment of a flat-rate ad hoc contribution to be determined. More information on what to do in the event of a claim can be found in the attachments to your travel grant allocation. Or you can contact AIG (EN: +32 (0)2 739 99 91; NL: +32 (0)2 739 99 92; FR: +32 (0)2 739 99 90).
The beneficiary must obtain a valid health and work accident insurance themselves.
* (3/04/2025)
The following countries cannot be insured: North Korea, Crimea, Russia and Belarus.
The following countries are considered 'dangerous regions and countries at war': Afghanistan; Gaza; The Golan Heights; Iraq; Israel; Lebanon; Somalia; Syria, Chechnya; Ukraine; West Bank. If cover is required in these countries, this must be requested in advance, approval must be given for this and an additional premium will be charged.
The following countries are sanctioned countries; Cuba, Iran and Syria. In principle, cover can be provided for this BUT no invoice from a local authority can be paid. You will therefore have to pay everything yourself here with any recovery afterwards.
Always contact the FWO if you travel to one of the above countries to discuss travel and assistance insurance in more detail.
Upon return
Reporting
Within three months of end date of travel credit, the beneficiary is required to submit a scientific report and financial justification (for the lump-sum daily allowance received). Do this using the report forms available on the dedicated FWO Travel grant website.

“Building a sustainable and innovative Blue Economy: mastering digital opportunities and fostering resilience”
On 14 September 2026, a fourth co-funded call for project funding was launched under the Horizon Europe Sustainable Blue Economy Partnership (SBEP) , which focuses on developing a climate-neutral, sustainable, productive and competitive blue economy.
This partnership, in which the Joint Programming Initiative (JPI) Oceans is closely involved, initiates a wide range of activities, including calls for research projects. As a research funder for Flanders, the FWO intends to participate in these calls, with an emphasis on funding Flemish research institutions and their researchers.
This new transnational call focuses on impact-driven research across the different European sea basins, within four priority areas:
Priority area 1: Transitioning the blue economy sectors for sustainability and strategic autonomy
Priority area 2: Managing sea-uses by advancing digital and ecosystem-based approaches
Priority area 3: Promoting sustainable blue bioresources through innovation in marine production and harvesting
Priority area 4: Building resilience in coastal communities with infrastructure and businesses
More information
Detailed information on the above themes can be found in the call document (‘call announcement’), available on the call page .
The call document also sets out the general participation guidelines and eligibility criteria for this two-stage call (i.e. the pre-proposal and full proposal stages). In addition, the FWO eligibility criteria also apply. These are included in the ‘National Contact Points and National/Regional Regulations’ document (ANNEX B; see from p. 9 onwards) and are relevant to the eligibility of the Flemish subproject for FWO funding.

16 November 2026, 15.00 CET (Brussels)

FWO-modalities
FWO participation and funding under this call cover both the Fundamental Research (FO) and Strategic Basic Research (SBO) funding channels, which means that projects may be funded under either FWO channel. It should be strongly emphasised that the choice of funding channel depends on the type of research project to be carried out (i.e. fundamental/exploratory in nature versus having strong potential for economic and/or societal utilisation in a Flemish context). Failure to comply with the specific requirements of the selected funding channel may result in the application being declared ineligible.
Please note that this is assessed on an individual basis: European and/or international partners within the same consortium are free to choose the type of research they conduct. From the FWO’s perspective, only the ‘nature’ of the subproject of the Flemish partner applying for FWO funding is relevant.
For the Fundamental Research funding channel, the relevant regulations apply. For the SBO funding channel, the SBO regulations must be taken into account. The latter concern projects with a primarily economic and/or societal objective, for which utilisation is an essential and mandatory component.
Submission
Research proposals must be submitted to the SBEP Call Secretariat by consortia of European and international researchers via the submission platform , in accordance with the applicable procedures and eligibility criteria set out in the aforementioned call documents. A partner search tool is also available to identify relevant consortium partners. Templates for the pre-proposal and full proposal are also available on the call page , together with other relevant documents.
Mandatory administrative FWO pre-registration
Researchers from Flanders applying to the FWO for funding must, in addition to the SBEP project proposal, submit an administrative application during the pre-proposal stage via the FWO e-portal :
For FO projects, select the application type ‘Research projects – European programme fundamental research’;
For SBO projects, select the application type ‘Strategic basic research projects (SBO)’.
Where several Flemish institutions applying for FWO funding collaborate within the same consortium, only one joint form must be submitted, containing all information on the Flemish parties involved, in line with the arrangements for regular projects. This must always be done by the PI/supervisor using their personal e-portal.
The submission deadline is the same as the call deadline for pre-proposals. Applicants are strongly advised to contact the FWO administration no later than one week before submission so that eligibility can be verified.
Call budget
Within international consortia, the FWO can support at least two Flemish projects. A maximum of €350,000 per project, including overhead, may be requested for a duration of 36 months. The FWO budgetary rules are also explained in the ‘National Contact Points and National/Regional Regulations’ document (ANNEX B; see from p. 9 onwards).
The co-funded partnerships under the Horizon Europe Framework Programme combine national and regional resources with co-funding from the European Commission. Following the international peer review and final ranking, this co-funding may provide additional support, enabling further projects to be funded, including in Flanders.
Key points and deviations from the ‘regular’ FWO participatory framework
Key points and deviations from the regular FWO participation framework to be taken into account:
Participation in European co-funded partnerships does not count towards the maximum number of regular FWO projects that may be applied for or held concurrently. However, for each call, a researcher may participate in no more than two different projects or consortia, provided that they address different topics. Under this SBEP call, a researcher may also act as consortium coordinator in only one project proposal.
Different overhead arrangements apply to the Fundamental Research and SBO funding channels. These are explained in the ‘National Contact Points and National/Regional Regulations’ document (ANNEX B; see from p. 9 onwards).
SBO projects aimed at developing a spin-off are not eligible in this context. This option may therefore not be described in the FWO pre-registration.
A user committee or advisory committee is not mandatory for an SBO project in this context.
Projects have a maximum duration of 36 months and must be budgeted accordingly. Automatic extensions are not permitted under this participation framework. Consequently, Article 28 of the regulations governing FWO research projects and Article 14 of the regulations governing SBO projects are explicitly excluded.
Researchers must hold an eligible appointment covering the full project duration. For FO projects, and more specifically with regard to the emeritus provision ( Article 10, §7 ), this provision does not apply in this context.
Research teams applying to the FWO for funding may, if desired, involve stakeholders (e.g. urban/municipal authorities and public and/or private organisations focusing on the blue economy in the broadest sense) in a research project, including for non-routine tasks. The state aid rules must nevertheless be respected. Contact the FWO for further information.
Researchers are strongly advised to contact the FWO regarding the eligibility criteria before submission. This is to avoid the individual Flemish researcher(s), and consequently the consortium as a whole, being declared ineligible.

“Novel ecosystems: biodiversity, socio-ecological consequences and future trajectories”
On 9 September 2026, a sixth co-funded call for project funding was launched under the Horizon Europe Biodiversa+ Partnership , focusing on biodiversity research. This large-scale European partnership undertakes a wide range of activities, including calls for research projects. As a research funder, the FWO is also participating in this new call and intends to fund Flemish universities and public research institutions and their researchers.
BiodivFuture aims to support forward-looking research and innovation on biodiversity and novel ecosystems, including their socio-ecological dynamics and consequences in relation to the sustainability objectives of the Kunming–Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework and the associated future development pathways and trajectories of change.
BiodivFuture’s research priorities are divided into three non-mutually exclusive themes. Projects may therefore address one or more themes:
THEME A – NOVEL ECOSYSTEM FUNCTIONING : Understanding the functioning of novel ecosystems and their links to socio-cultural and socio-economic dynamics, identifying mechanisms and developing approaches that can facilitate biodiversity gains.
THEME B – PATHWAYS TO NOVEL ECOSYSTEMS : Studying robust, plausible, and concrete pathways to novel ecosystems and associated socio-ecological dynamics to prosper under temporal asynchronies and uncertainties.
THEME C – BIODIVERSITY BENEFITS AND EQUITY IN THE CONTEXT OF NOVEL ECOSYSTEM: Studying fair and equitable sharing of biodiversity benefits in the context of novel ecosystems, supported by inclusive, collaborative, and investment-oriented pathways for nature-positive socio-ecological transformation.
More information
Further details on the above topics are available in the ‘Announcement of Opportunity’ document and on the BiodivFuture call page .
The full ‘Announcement of Opportunity’ document also sets out the general participation guidelines and eligibility criteria for this two-stage call (i.e. the pre-proposal and full-proposal stages). The FWO eligibility criteria also apply. These are included in the ‘Funding Organisation Rules’ document (see p. 12 onwards) and determine the eligibility of the Flemish subproject for FWO funding.
Important note:
FWO and BELSPO funding cannot be applied for simultaneously within the same consortium, even if the applications concern different research institutions.

10 November 2026, 12.00 CET (Brussels)

FWO-modalities
FWO participation and funding under this call cover both the fundamental research (FO) and Strategic Basic Research (SBO) funding channels, meaning that projects may be funded through either FWO channel. It must be strongly emphasised that the choice of funding channel depends on the type of research project to be conducted (i.e. fundamental/exploratory research versus research strongly geared towards future economic or societal valorisation potential). Failure to comply with the specific requirements of the selected funding channel may render an application ineligible.
Please note that this is assessed individually: European and/or international partners within the same consortium may choose their type of research freely. From the FWO’s perspective, only the nature of the subproject undertaken by the Flemish partner applying to the FWO for funding is relevant.
The relevant regulations apply to the fundamental research funding channel . For the SBO funding channel, the SBO regulations must be taken into account. SBO projects have a primary economic and/or societal purpose, and valorisation is an essential and mandatory component.
Submission
Research proposals must be submitted by consortia of European and international researchers to the Biodiversa call secretariat via the ‘ EPSS submission platform ’, in accordance with the applicable procedures and eligibility criteria set out in the call documents referred to above. A ‘partner search tool’ is also available to help identify suitable consortium partners. Examples of the pre-proposal and full-proposal templates are likewise available on the BiodivFuture call page .
New: FWO administrative pre-registration
Researchers from Flanders applying to the FWO for funding must, in addition to the Biodiversa project proposal, submit an administrative application during the pre-proposal stage via the FWO e-portal , using the personal e-portal account of the Flemish principal investigator acting as consortium spokesperson.
For FO projects, select the application type ‘Research projects – European programme fundamental research’;
For SBO projects, select the application type ‘Strategic Basic Research projects (SBO)’.
Where several Flemish institutions within the same consortium apply for FWO funding, only one joint form containing all information on the Flemish parties concerned must be submitted, in line with the procedure for regular projects. This must always be done by the principal investigator or principal investigator acting as spokesperson through their personal e-portal account.
The submission deadline is the same as the call deadline for pre-proposals. Applicants are strongly advised to contact the FWO administration no later than one week before submission so that eligibility can be verified.
Call budget
Within an international consortium, the FWO will support at least three Flemish projects, with a maximum amount of €250,000 (including overheads) available per project for a period of 36 months. The FWO budget rules are also explained in the ‘Funding Organisation Rules’ document (see p. 12 onwards).
In addition to national and regional resources, co-funded partnerships under the Horizon Europe Framework Programme receive co-funding from the European Commission. Following international peer review, this may result in additional support at the funding-decision stage on the basis of the final ranking, enabling further projects to be funded, including projects from Flanders.
Key points and deviations from the ‘regular’ FWO participatory framework
Key points and departures from the FWO’s regular participation framework to be taken into account:
FWO and BELSPO funding cannot be applied for simultaneously within the same consortium, even if the applications concern different research institutions.
Participation in European co-funded partnerships does not affect participation in ‘regular’ or ‘national’ FWO projects and is therefore not counted towards the maximum number of projects that may be applied for or held concurrently. However, an applicant may participate in no more than two different projects/consortia per call, provided that they address different topics. In this Biodiversa+ context, an applicant may also act as ‘Project Partner Coordinator’ only once.
Different overhead arrangements apply to the fundamental research and SBO funding channels, as explained in the ‘Funding Organisation Rules’ document (see p. 12 onwards).
SBO projects aimed at developing a spin-off are not eligible in this context. This option may therefore not be described in the FWO pre-registration.
A user committee or ‘advisory committee’ is not mandatory for an SBO project in this context.
Projects may run for a maximum of 36 months and must be budgeted accordingly. Automatic extensions are not permitted under this participation framework. Consequently, Article 28 of the f undamental FWO Research Projects Regulations and Article 14 of the SBO Projects Regulations are expressly excluded.
Researchers must hold an eligible appointment covering the full duration of the project. For FO projects, the emeritus provisions in Article 10(§7) do not apply in this context. In other words, (future) emeritus professors are not eligible.
Research teams applying to the FWO for funding may, if they wish, involve stakeholders (e.g. nature conservation organisations, interest groups, municipal or city authorities, and public and/or private organisations focusing on biodiversity) in a research project, including in non-routine tasks. Applicants must, however, take account of the state-aid rules. Please contact the FWO for further information if required.
Researchers are strongly advised to contact the FWO before submission regarding the eligibility criteria. This will help avoid the Flemish researcher or researchers, and the consortium as a whole, being declared ineligible.
