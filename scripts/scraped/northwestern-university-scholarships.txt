Apply for Aid
The Office of Undergraduate Financial Aid is here to help you apply for financial aid, whether you are a prospective student, a newly admitted student, or a continuing student.
Application Process Overview
Use these general steps as a starting guide before choosing your student group below for specific deadlines and required forms:
Indicate your status when applying for admission.
When completing the application for admission, be sure to indicate that you are requesting consideration for financial aid.
Complete and submit your FAFSA (Domestic students only).
The Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA) is the official application for federal and many state financial aid programs. Northwestern's School Code is 001739 .
Submit your CSS Profile through The College Board website.
The College Scholarship Service (CSS) Profile is completed and submitted online through The College Board website. Northwestern's CSS Profile code is 1565 .
Complete and submit federal tax forms.
You will receive information from the College Board's Institutional Documentation Service with instructions for submitting your tax forms and required supplemental forms.
Receive for award notification and respond.
Your financial aid award notification will be sent with or soon after your acceptance letter. If you qualify for loans, you will be notified and emailed instructions on how to complete any additional loan materials online.
Detailed Timelines
Select Your Application Group
Please be sure to follow deadlines to ensure you receive your financial aid award in a timely manner.
Choose your&#160;admission application group to view specific deadlines, required forms, and step-by-step instructions.
Contact us with any questions or if you need assistance.

Sample Financial Aid Letter
Requirements
Northwestern University offers financial assistance to students on the basis of demonstrated need.
Northwestern is among a small group of institutions that continue to meet the full demonstrated university-defined financial need of our domestic aid applicants.
If you fulfill the other academic and citizenship requirements , your eligibility for aid will come down to your family's financial situation.
Please note:&#160; If you're an international or undocumented student , your eligibility and aid considerations will be different. Please visit Resources for International Students for more information.
How We Determine Your Financial Aid Eligibility
Your eligibility for need-based financial aid is based on the difference between Cost of Attendance and your Family Contribution:
Cost of Attendance &#8211; Family Contribution = Demonstrated Financial Need
Step 1: We Start with Cost of Attendance
Your Cost of Attendance includes estimated expenses such as tuition and fees, housing and meals, books and supplies, personal expenses and transportation. See a detailed estimate for a full cost of attendance .
Step 2: We Calculate Your Family Contribution
Your Family Contribution is calculated by the financial aid office based on information provided on your&#160;FAFSA,&#160;CSS Profile (if required), and supporting financial documents.
Family contribution considers:
Student contribution: Students are expected to contribute to their educational costs from their own savings/assets and from their income.
Parent contribution: The parent contribution is calculated based on income and assets compared to the household size and any siblings concurrently enrolled in college.
Special considerations: We also note any special circumstances reported on your aid application, which may be considered in the calculation of family contribution.
Step 3: We Calculate Your Demonstrated Financial Need
Once we determine your Family Contribution, we compare it to your Cost of Attendance to determine your financial need. That figure is used to calculate the amount of financial aid your qualify for.&#160;
You can get an estimate of the aid you'll receive at any time by using our quick estimator tools:
Estimate Your Cost and Aid
Will I qualify for need-based aid?
Generally:
If your Family Contribution is less than your Cost of Attendance, you may qualify for need-based financial aid.
If your Family Contribution is equal to or greater than the Cost of Attendance, you will not qualify for need-based aid.
Students who do not qualify for need-based aid may still have access to other financing options, including:
Unsubsidized Stafford Loans
Parent PLUS Loans
Private Loans
Is my family contribution how much I'll own Northwestern?
Your family contribution is not necessarily the amount you will owe Northwestern. Your family contribution is an estimate of your family's total contribution each quarter or year, and financial aid is provided after your family contribution is first applied. If your financial aid package includes employment, this assistance will be paid to you in the form of paychecks for hours worked, and these funds can help you to pay for your books and personal expenses.
If your financial aid package doesn't cover all the directly charged costs on the University bill, then your family contribution will cover the remainder of the bill as well as the indirect costs (out-of-pocket expenses).
If your amount of direct financial aid covers only the directly charged costs, then your family contribution is expected to be used for those indirect or out-of-pocket expenses.
If the amount of your direct aid exceeds the amount of your University bill, you will have a credit balance, or financial aid refund , which you can use to help with your other expenses.
Will my family contribution be the same every quarter or year?
Your estimated family contribution is the same each quarter of the academic year.
You will need to apply for financial aid every year, but if your family's finances remain similar from year to year (e.g., same income/asset level, same number of dependents enrolled concurrently as undergraduates, etc.), you can expect a similar aid package each year.
How do we pay our family contribution?
Families cover their educational costs through income, savings, payment plans, education loans, or a combination of these things.
If you are considering borrowing to replace all or part of your family contribution, we encourage you to borrow wisely and only what you need. For example, if you are able to pay some expenses "out-of-pocket," such as books or transportation costs, you may be able to borrow a smaller amount, and ultimately pay less in loan fees or interest long-term. If you need assistance, please contact our office to discuss your budget and determine an amount.
What do we do if we can't pay our contribution?
If you are unable to manage the expected contribution due to changes in their financial circumstances (e.g., job loss, income reduction, etc.), you might request a Special Circumstances Review . Feel free to contact our office and speak with a financial aid counselor to discuss your circumstances prior to filing your special circumstances application.
What costs does financial aid cover?
Financial aid can be used to help with all the costs listed in the cost of attendance &#8211; not just the directly charged costs.
Financial aid might include scholarships, grants, loans, and part-time student employment (e.g., work-study).
Scholarships, grants, and loans are called "direct aid," and these will apply directly to your bill at Northwestern. Your bill will include your tuition, fees, and housing and meals if you live in University housing. In other words, your "direct aid" applies to your "direct costs" first. If there is a surplus of direct aid on the bill, this surplus may be used for indirect costs through the financial aid refund process.
Students who utilize work-study aid may use their earnings to assist with indirect costs, or save the funds to help submit payments towards the next term's directly charged costs on the student bill.
What is an "Unusual Circumstance"?
In some unfortunate cases, a student might have suffered in some way such that they have been separated from their parent or parents. Federal guidance defines these cases as "Unusual Circumstances". In these cases, it might be necessary that a student is determined to be "independent" for purposes of federal financial aid. Federal guidance provides examples such as human trafficking, refugee or asylee status, parental abandonment, incarceration of parents...etc. Please contact the office if you are in such a circumstance.

Requirements
Educational loans can be borrowed by a student or their parent to help with costs not covered by other sources of aid. Unlike scholarships or grants, loans must be paid back.
At Northwestern, scholarships, grants, and work-study cover 100% of demonstrated financial need . Some families choose loans as a way to fund their expected parent or student contribution. In general, students and parents can wait until the summer before the fall term begins to apply for additional loans and complete requirements. Each student loan has different terms and conditions. Before applying or accepting student loans, take some time to research your options.
Please note: If you're an international or undocumented student , your eligibility and aid considerations will be different. Please visit Resources for International Students for more information.
Financial Aid Package: Questions to Ask
What will my financial aid package include?
A student who receives need-based financial aid from Northwestern University may see Northwestern Scholarships as a component of their financial aid package. Domestic students who demonstrate need may see federal grants or work-study, if they qualify for these programs. Finally, students who are residents of Illinois and demonstrate need may see state grants on their aid package.
The financial aid components listed above will meet a student's demonstrated financial need . You can learn more about demonstrated need on our Financial Need and Eligibility page , and you can use our calculators to get an estimate of what your need may be.
Also, a complete financial aid package may later include outside scholarships (which students must report to Northwestern ), and student loans (if the student or parents are using loans as a part of their Estimated Family Contribution).
What if I still need financial support beyond my aid award?
Students or their parents can utilize educational loans to help finance their contribution.
If a family experiences a change that impacts their financial situation, they should reach out to our office to explore the Special Circumstances Review process .
Loans: Planning Ahead
Our student loan page is a great starting point for this research, and we'll guide you to other helpful resources along the way. Also, admitted and current students are always welcome to make an appointment with a financial aid counselor to discuss this process further. We're here to help!
Questions you should ask a potential lender
It is important to ask the following questions if you are considering any private loans:
Does this loan application require a credit review or credit check?
Do I need a cosigner or comaker to apply for and receive this loan?
When does interest begin accruing?
Am I expected to make payments while in school?
What is the interest rate? Can this interest rate change over time, or can it be adjusted with a cosigner?
What will my monthly payment be when I enter repayment? What kinds of payment plans might I qualify for to help with the payments?
How soon will I enter repayment? Are interest-only payments required during enrollment?
How often is the interest capitalized? (The frequency of capitalization will increase the total loan amount that you have to repay because you will be charged interest on a higher principal loan.)
If I use a co-signer, can I receive a better rate?
How will the rate formula (interest and fees charged) change in repayment?
What will my monthly payment be on this loan amount when I am in repayment?
What kind of borrower benefits does the lender offer? (interest rate reductions with automatic withdrawals, principal reduction with a certain number of on-time payments)
Important considerations before applying
Before applying for these loans, it is important for you to ensure that you have reviewed all your federal loan options and eligibility.
Important note: Prospective borrowers should note that they may qualify for loans or other assistance under the federal Title IV programs and the terms and conditions of loans under the federal Title IV programs may be more favorable than those of private education loans.
Why consider federal loans first?
Federal loans generally have better benefits and lower, fixed interest rates.
We encourage you to consider the Federal PLUS/Grad PLUS Loan (depending on your academic level) before applying for a private loan.
Before you apply to a private loan:
Students are encouraged to review the Department of Education's information on Federal Versus Private Loans and speak to their financial aid office to learn about the Federal Loan programs before you apply for a private educational loan.
Undergraduate students applying for federal financial aid may also be eligible for a Federal Pell Grant.
Required forms for private loan applications
Your private education lender will require you to complete a Private Education Loan Applicant Self-Certification Form .
Step 1: Obtain the form from your lender
You should use the version the lender provides as it may contain pre-populated information.
Step 2: Use the backup version if needed
You may use this version if your lender does not provide you with one.
Step 3: Gather your financial information
For applicants receiving other forms of aid: Information necessary for completing this form, such as cost of attendance and financial assistance received, is available to you via CAESAR > View Financial Aid.
Step 4: Contact Financial Aid for help
Applicants can also contact our office to speak to a financial aid counselor to retrieve this information.

Maximum Timeframe Requirements
Students receive their aid offer based on demonstrated financial need and eligibility for federal and state aid programs.
The aid students are offered comes with certain requirements that must be met for students to receive their aid. Read more below about requirements and responsibilities for students to receive financial aid for their undergraduate education.
Keep in mind: a variety of other financing options, like loans, are available for those who are not eligible for need-based aid.
Academic Requirements
In addition to financial need, to be eligible for institutional assistance, students must be:&#160;
Working toward first bachelor's degree
Enrolled at least half-time at Northwestern
Maintaining Satisfactory Academic Progress
Within the Maximum Timeframe Requirements
Citizenship Requirements
In order to be eligible for federal student aid, students must meet the above requirements and they must be U.S. citizens or eligible non-citizens .
Loan Requirements
If a student or parent borrows federal student loans, they are beholden to all the above requirements, and they are required to complete:&#160;
Entrance counseling online through the StudentAid.gov website
Master Promissory Note through the StudentAid.gov website
Exit counseling through StudentAid.gov website before graduation or leaving the university
Outside Scholarships
A student who has an outside scholarship is required to report the scholarship to our office .
Northwestern will not enter into a financial or consortium agreement with another institution for the purposes of processing financial assistance.

The scholarships and grants featured on our site are given to students who demonstrate financial need . This type of funding does not need to be repaid.
Northwestern Scholarships and Grants
Scholarships from Northwestern are need-based, often made up of donor funding, and are awarded to approximately 45% of undergrads each year. Northwestern Scholarships and Grants
Federal and State Scholarships and Grants
Domestic students with high financial need may qualify for federally funded aid like Pell Grants and Federal SEOG. Students from Illinois who have high need may also qualify for the Illinois MAP Grant. Federal and State Scholarships and Grants
Outside Scholarships
Outside scholarships are funds awarded by entities apart from the federal, state, and institutional aid programs. Outside Scholarships
Report Your Scholarships
If you receive an outside scholarship, you are required to report it to our office so we can post the funds to your student account.
