Norad - The Norwegian Agency for Development Cooperation
Norad is an administrative and specialist body for development cooperation and supports the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the Ministry of Climate and the Environment in achieving the goals of Norwegian development policy and promoting sustainable development. The UN&#x27;s sustainability goals, climate goals and human rights form the basis of Norad&#x27;s work. Norad is based in Oslo and has around 320 employees.

Nepal and China: Complex relief efforts after the landslide
After the flash flood struck the border areas between Nepal and China, the death toll is rising rapidly. Norway is supporting the relief effort with NOK 10 million from the humanitarian reserve, in addition to support through the UN and Red Cross emergency funds.
Yemen: Severe hunger and malnutrition
The humanitarian crisis in Yemen continues to deteriorate. The UN reports that more than half of the population is facing acute food insecurity.
Stronger protection against mines and explosive ordnance
To protect civilians from landmines and explosive remnants of war, Norway is contributing nearly NOK 1 billion to six organisations.

The Nansen Support Programme for Ukraine
The Nansen Programme for Ukraine is a Norwegian support programme for both civilian and military aid, with a total budget of NOK 275 billion for the period 2023–2030.
Disturbing trend for humanitarian workers in Ukraine
– It has become more dangerous to be a humanitarian worker in Ukraine. Last week, two deminers from Norwegian People&#x27;s Aid were killed. This week, the Ukrainian Red Cross&#x27;s main warehouse in Kyiv was completely destroyed in a Russian attack, says Norad Director General Gunn Jorid Roset.
Ukraine: Fighting to Protect World Heritage
On Monday, 15 June, the more than 1,000-year-old Kyiv Pechersk Lavra Monastery caught fire after being struck by a Russian missile. Norway stands with Ukraine in protecting and rebuilding World Heritage sites and cultural heritage.
Ukrainian journalists expose corruption in wartime
Together with independent anti-corruption agencies and civil society, a free and independent press plays a central role in the fight against corruption.

Sudan: Looking to the future amid the crisis
During a highly challenging humanitarian situation, work on long-term measures continues. For many, it is about the possibility of looking ahead, even in the middle of the crisis. The Norwegian Refugee Council is helping to ensure that Hanaa and other women can gain greater control over their own lives.
Gaza: – Hard to comprehend how badly injured the children are
Red Cross doctor Roger Alcock has never seen so many injured people as in Gaza. At the field hospital in Rafah, they save lives and also create moments of joy for the children in the midst of the catastrophe.
Standing Together Against Illegal Gold Mining in the Amazon
In the heart of the Amazon, authorities and Indigenous peoples are joining forces in the fight against illegal gold mining.
They support each other in the fight against corruption
«Why shouldn’t we have our own global network when the criminals have theirs?», said Eva Joly when she launched the international Corruption Hunter Network in 2005 – a network led and supported by Norad.
Historic agreement for women shea workers in Ghana
Hardworking and underpaid women in Ghana are now seeing their labour rights strengthened. The country’s first collective agreement in the shea sector has been signed, with support from Norway.
The world’s most important backup: seeds for the future
Deep in the permafrost of Svalbard, more than one million seed samples are stored – a global insurance policy in case vital plant species disappear. At the same time, farmers in Somalia are testing new seed varieties to strengthen food production in the face of climate change and conflict.

This section on Norad&rsquo;s webpages provides potential applicants for funding from Norad and existing grant recipients with an overview of the main expectations as well as legal and financial rules applicable to project/programme grants from Norad. These pages are relevant for civil society organisations and other private actors such as research institutes and private sector entities.
Note that the information on this webpage is not exhaustive and applicants are therefore asked to carefully review any documentation shared by Norad, specifically grant agreements, and direct communication with Norad staff. The grant agreement constitutes the legal basis for a grant. This text in these Guidelines is not legally binding.

Grant scheme rules are a set of rules describing how the Norwegian Govenment's National Budget (Prop 1 S) should be operationalised in connection with allocation of funds to grant recipients. Calls for Proposals by Norad hence have to be aligned with the grant scheme rules for the relevant budget post. Norad is responsible for drafting and revising the grant scheme rules for budget posts that have been either fully or partially delegated from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs to Norad. The Ministry approved them.

Nepal and China: Complex relief efforts after the landslide

Published yesterday
Updated yesterday
Updated yesterday

Bildetekst
Photo: Prabin Ranabhat/AFP/NTB
Aerial photo of houses buried in mud after the flood in Devighat, in Nepal’s Nuwakot District, which began on 26 August 2026.
Photo: Prabin Ranabhat/AFP/NTB
After the flash flood struck the border areas between Nepal and China, the death toll is rising rapidly. Norway is supporting the relief effort with NOK 10 million from the humanitarian reserve, in addition to support through the UN and Red Cross emergency funds.
The flood followed either a glacial lake outburst or a landslide in the Himalayas, which set large volumes of water in motion. The hardest-hit areas are the districts of Rasuwa and Nuwak
ot in Nepal, and Gyirong County on the Chinese side.
Norway is contributing NOK 10 million to the relief effort from its flood response reserve. These are funds allocated at the start of the year and can be used where they are needed most.
&ndash; When a crisis strikes, the first hours and days are critical. That is why the funds must be flexible so that assistance can get under way as quickly as possible, says Martin Samuelsen, Head of Section for Humanitarian Assistance at Norad.
Martin Samuelsen is Head of Section for Humanitarian Assistance at Norad.
Photo: Fartein Rudjord/Norad
The flash flood struck with little warning and has destroyed homes, roads, bridges, hydropower facilities and other critical infrastructure. Several of the worst-affected areas are now extremely difficult to access.
The number of people confirmed dead or missing is rising rapidly, and it remains difficult to establish a full overview of the situation. In Nepal, at least 289 people have been confirmed dead, while several hundred remain missing. Among those missing are more than 500 foreign nationals from 32 countries, including India, the United States, Australia, the United Kingdom, Ukraine, Malaysia and Canada. On the Chinese side, information remains more limited, but at least three deaths have been confirmed and around 558 people are missing, including 260 foreign nationals. No Norwegian citizens are among the missing.
International response
The response in Nepal is being led by the Nepali authorities (NDRRMA), in cooperation with the UN, the Nepal Red Cross/IFRC and international organisations.
On the Chinese side, the response is being led by the Chinese authorities. The UN office in China stands ready to provide support, but no international humanitarian organisations are operating directly in the affected areas on the Chinese side.
Norway is supporting the response through the Red Cross&rsquo;s Disaster Response Emergency Fund (DREF) and the UN Central Emergency Response Fund (CERF), in addition to the NOK 10 million from the humanitarian reserve. These financing mechanisms are designed to enable a rapid response when disasters strike.
DREF has allocated CHF 1 million, equivalent to approximately NOK 11.6 million.
CERF has allocated USD 2 million, equivalent to approximately NOK 18.5 million.
Norway is one of the largest contributors to DREF, providing around seven per cent of its funding. Norway is also one of the largest donors to CERF, financing approximately ten per cent of the fund.
Norad has not previously earmarked humanitarian funding for Nepal. At the same time, Nepal faces widespread poverty, major geographical disparities and high vulnerability to natural hazards. Many communities, particularly in mountainous areas, have limited access to basic services and infrastructure even under normal circumstances. When a disaster of this scale strikes, humanitarian needs can escalate rapidly.
Among the Norwegian-supported organisations working in the country, Save the Children is an example of a partner that receives Norwegian funding for development cooperation while also operating as a humanitarian actor. Save the Children is, among other things, engaged in coordination efforts as part of the response.
