Undercover in a Russian troll factory
Russia attempted to influence Moldova’s parliamentary elections through digital warfare. By infiltrating pro-Russian networks controlled from Moscow, Moldovan journalist Natalia Zaharescu was able to expose how troll factories operate.

Norad - The Norwegian Agency for Development Cooperation
Norad is an administrative and specialist body for development cooperation and supports the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the Ministry of Climate and the Environment in achieving the goals of Norwegian development policy and promoting sustainable development. The UN&#x27;s sustainability goals, climate goals and human rights form the basis of Norad&#x27;s work. Norad is based in Oslo and has around 320 employees.

Critical water shortage in Gaza
Norway is providing NOK 25 million to UNICEF from the humanitarian reserve to maintain critical water, sanitation and hygiene services in Gaza.
– All girls have the right to decide over their own bodies and futures
Norway is stepping up its efforts to improve women’s health and is providing NOK 210 million to UNFPA Supplies. The funding will help ensure better access to contraception, family planning and life-saving health services for young women in developing countries.
The grant scheme for Civil Society Strengthening is changing
The main objectives are to shift power and influence to local actors in the Global South, achieve better results and more efficient management. Here you will find updated information about the changes being implemented for the grant scheme for Civil Society strengthening.
Kenya’s «Special Forces» – from stigma to innovation
A disability can also be a strength and a driving force for innovation and job creation. A group of young entrepreneurs in Kenya demonstrate this clearly. With support from a Norwegian-funded incubator, they are developing solutions for transport, communication and participation.
Norad supports the struggle for protection for land and environmental defenders
Those who defend nature, the environment and Indigenous peoples’ rights are increasingly exposed to threats, harassment and violence. At least 124 environmental defenders were killed last year, and since 2012, more than 2,000 environmental defenders have been killed, according to a new report.

The Nansen Support Programme for Ukraine
The Nansen Programme for Ukraine is a Norwegian support programme for both civilian and military aid, with a total budget of NOK 275 billion for the period 2023–2030.
Ukraine: Working to secure justice for 114,000 missing persons
No war in Europe since the Second World War has left as many people missing as the war in Ukraine. Some 114,000 Ukrainians, civilians and military personnel, children and adults, have been reported missing. Their loved ones are searching for answers.
– Every morning begins with a new challenge
Without a stable electricity supply, many businesses and jobs in Ukraine are at risk. With support from Norway, many companies are now investing in their own power supply.
Ukraine: Fighting to Protect World Heritage
On Monday, 15 June, the more than 1,000-year-old Kyiv Pechersk Lavra Monastery caught fire after being struck by a Russian missile. Norway stands with Ukraine in protecting and rebuilding World Heritage sites and cultural heritage.

Sudan: Looking to the future amid the crisis
During a highly challenging humanitarian situation, work on long-term measures continues. For many, it is about the possibility of looking ahead, even in the middle of the crisis. The Norwegian Refugee Council is helping to ensure that Hanaa and other women can gain greater control over their own lives.
Gaza: – Hard to comprehend how badly injured the children are
Red Cross doctor Roger Alcock has never seen so many injured people as in Gaza. At the field hospital in Rafah, they save lives and also create moments of joy for the children in the midst of the catastrophe.
Standing Together Against Illegal Gold Mining in the Amazon
In the heart of the Amazon, authorities and Indigenous peoples are joining forces in the fight against illegal gold mining.
They support each other in the fight against corruption
«Why shouldn’t we have our own global network when the criminals have theirs?», said Eva Joly when she launched the international Corruption Hunter Network in 2005 – a network led and supported by Norad.
Historic agreement for women shea workers in Ghana
Hardworking and underpaid women in Ghana are now seeing their labour rights strengthened. The country’s first collective agreement in the shea sector has been signed, with support from Norway.
The world’s most important backup: seeds for the future
Deep in the permafrost of Svalbard, more than one million seed samples are stored – a global insurance policy in case vital plant species disappear. At the same time, farmers in Somalia are testing new seed varieties to strengthen food production in the face of climate change and conflict.

This section on Norad&rsquo;s webpages provides potential applicants for funding from Norad and existing grant recipients with an overview of the main expectations as well as legal and financial rules applicable to project/programme grants from Norad. These pages are relevant for civil society organisations and other private actors such as research institutes and private sector entities.
Note that the information on this webpage is not exhaustive and applicants are therefore asked to carefully review any documentation shared by Norad, specifically grant agreements, and direct communication with Norad staff. The grant agreement constitutes the legal basis for a grant. This text in these Guidelines is not legally binding.

Grant scheme rules are a set of rules describing how the Norwegian Govenment's National Budget (Prop 1 S) should be operationalised in connection with allocation of funds to grant recipients. Calls for Proposals by Norad hence have to be aligned with the grant scheme rules for the relevant budget post. Norad is responsible for drafting and revising the grant scheme rules for budget posts that have been either fully or partially delegated from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs to Norad. The Ministry approved them.

– All girls have the right to decide over their own bodies and futures
Published 9/28/2026

Bildetekst
Photo: Thoko Chikondi/UNFPA
The UNFPA Ndenguma Safe Space project in Nsanje District in southern Malawi gives young people access to sexual and reproductive health education and contraception. The aim is to prevent child marriage and teenage pregnancy.
Photo: Thoko Chikondi/UNFPA
Norway is stepping up its efforts to improve women’s health and is providing NOK 210 million to UNFPA Supplies. The funding will help ensure better access to contraception, family planning and life-saving health services for young women in developing countries.
The support for the United Nations Population Fund comes at a time when women&rsquo;s rights are being challenged in many parts of the world, while international funding for health is under pressure.
&ndash; Far too many young women around the world lack what we in Norway often take for granted: the freedom to decide over their own bodies and their own futures. That is why Norwegian support for UNFPA is particularly important, says Norad Director General Gunn Jorid Roset.
Norad Director General Gunn Jorid Roset and UNFPA Deputy Executive Director Diene Keita during the 2026 UN General Assembly, discussing the importance of protecting women rights at a challenging time.
Photo: Amalie Henriksen/Norad
The government announced the new agreement with the United Nations Population Fund UNFPA during this week&rsquo;s United Nations General Assembly. Norway will provide a total of NOK 210 million to the UNFPA Supplies Partnership over the next two years.
Norway stands up for women&rsquo;s rights
&ndash; As opposition to women&rsquo;s rights increases, it is especially important that countries like Norway stand up for what works. No woman should lose her life because she lacks access to contraception, safe abortion or healthcare during pregnancy and childbirth, says Norway&rsquo;s Minister of International Development &Aring;smund Aukrust.
Minister of International Development Åsmund Aukrust.
Photo: Fartein Rudjord/Norad
Norway is a global advocate for sexual and reproductive health and rights (SRHR), and UNFPA is Norway&rsquo;s most important UN partner in this area.
The UNFPA Supplies Partnership is a global fund that helps countries ensure access to contraception and other essential sexual and reproductive health services. The partnership strengthens health systems and gives young people, in particular, the opportunity to make informed choices about their own bodies and futures.
Contraception and safe abortion save lives
Norad is responsible for following up the agreement with UNFPA Supplies and represents Norway on its board. Norad also provides important technical input on how the funding can reach the poorest people.
We know that access to contraception saves lives, prevents unintended pregnancies and gives women and girls the opportunity to decide over their own bodies and futures. This is a prerequisite for gender equality, good health and sustainable development for society as a whole, emphasises Gunn Jorid Roset.
Norway has a clear voice on the UNFPA Supplies board and is listened to. For example, access to safe abortion is an issue Norad consistently raises in meetings.
She also emphasises that access to information and modern contraception helps reduce the number of people infected with sexually transmitted infections, including HIV, limits unintended pregnancies, and reduces maternal and child mortality. At the same time, families have better opportunities to provide their children with a safe upbringing and ensure access to education, employment opportunities and a better financial situation.

Norwegian support for UNFPA Supplies
Norway is providing NOK 210 million to the UNFPA Supplies Partnership in 2026 and 2027.
UNFPA Supplies is the largest supplier of contraceptive commodities to low- and middle-income countries.
Norway has provided NOK 443 million to the partnership over the past five years.
Norway also provides NOK 589.6 million in annual core funding to UNFPA.
Norad manages the funding and represents Norway on the board.
