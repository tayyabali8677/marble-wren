Nepal and China: Enormous humanitarian needs
The flood disaster has claimed at least 1,250 lives. Norway is supporting the highly demanding relief effort with NOK 10 million from the humanitarian reserve, in addition to support through the UN and Red Cross emergency funds.

Norad - The Norwegian Agency for Development Cooperation
Norad is an administrative and specialist body for development cooperation and supports the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the Ministry of Climate and the Environment in achieving the goals of Norwegian development policy and promoting sustainable development. The UN&#x27;s sustainability goals, climate goals and human rights form the basis of Norad&#x27;s work. Norad is based in Oslo and has around 320 employees.

Expanding solar power in South Africa with a Norwegian guarantee
With a state guarantee of up to NOK 150 million, Norway is contributing to the further development of South Africa’s power market and expansion of solar power for industrial use. The Norwegian company Scatec will build and operate the plant.
The grant scheme for Civil Society Strengthening is changing
The main objectives are to shift power and influence to local actors in the Global South, achieve better results and more efficient management. Here you will find updated information about the changes being implemented for the grant scheme for Civil Society strengthening.
Yemen: Severe hunger and malnutrition
The humanitarian crisis in Yemen continues to deteriorate. The UN reports that more than half of the population is facing acute food insecurity.

The Nansen Support Programme for Ukraine
The Nansen Programme for Ukraine is a Norwegian support programme for both civilian and military aid, with a total budget of NOK 275 billion for the period 2023–2030.
NOK 400 million for mine clearance in Ukraine
Large parts of Ukraine are either mined or contaminated with explosives. Norway is now scaling up its support to three organisations to help make land areas safer for both people and food production.
– Every morning begins with a new challenge
Without a stable electricity supply, many businesses and jobs in Ukraine are at risk. With support from Norway, many companies are now investing in their own power supply.
Ukraine: Fighting to Protect World Heritage
On Monday, 15 June, the more than 1,000-year-old Kyiv Pechersk Lavra Monastery caught fire after being struck by a Russian missile. Norway stands with Ukraine in protecting and rebuilding World Heritage sites and cultural heritage.

Sudan: Looking to the future amid the crisis
During a highly challenging humanitarian situation, work on long-term measures continues. For many, it is about the possibility of looking ahead, even in the middle of the crisis. The Norwegian Refugee Council is helping to ensure that Hanaa and other women can gain greater control over their own lives.
Gaza: – Hard to comprehend how badly injured the children are
Red Cross doctor Roger Alcock has never seen so many injured people as in Gaza. At the field hospital in Rafah, they save lives and also create moments of joy for the children in the midst of the catastrophe.
Standing Together Against Illegal Gold Mining in the Amazon
In the heart of the Amazon, authorities and Indigenous peoples are joining forces in the fight against illegal gold mining.
They support each other in the fight against corruption
«Why shouldn’t we have our own global network when the criminals have theirs?», said Eva Joly when she launched the international Corruption Hunter Network in 2005 – a network led and supported by Norad.
Historic agreement for women shea workers in Ghana
Hardworking and underpaid women in Ghana are now seeing their labour rights strengthened. The country’s first collective agreement in the shea sector has been signed, with support from Norway.
The world’s most important backup: seeds for the future
Deep in the permafrost of Svalbard, more than one million seed samples are stored – a global insurance policy in case vital plant species disappear. At the same time, farmers in Somalia are testing new seed varieties to strengthen food production in the face of climate change and conflict.

This section on Norad&rsquo;s webpages provides potential applicants for funding from Norad and existing grant recipients with an overview of the main expectations as well as legal and financial rules applicable to project/programme grants from Norad. These pages are relevant for civil society organisations and other private actors such as research institutes and private sector entities.
Note that the information on this webpage is not exhaustive and applicants are therefore asked to carefully review any documentation shared by Norad, specifically grant agreements, and direct communication with Norad staff. The grant agreement constitutes the legal basis for a grant. This text in these Guidelines is not legally binding.

Grant scheme rules are a set of rules describing how the Norwegian Govenment's National Budget (Prop 1 S) should be operationalised in connection with allocation of funds to grant recipients. Calls for Proposals by Norad hence have to be aligned with the grant scheme rules for the relevant budget post. Norad is responsible for drafting and revising the grant scheme rules for budget posts that have been either fully or partially delegated from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs to Norad. The Ministry approved them.

The grant scheme for Civil Society Strengthening is changing
Published 9/10/2026

Bildetekst
Photo: Fartein Rudjord/Norad
Norad and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs are restructuring civil society assistance to promote greater locally led development. The photo shows participants in the Female Future course in Nairobi in November 2025. Female Future Kenya is a flagship leadership development programme run by the Federation of Kenya Employers (FKE).
Photo: Fartein Rudjord/Norad
The main objectives are to shift power and influence to local actors in the Global South, achieve better results and more efficient management. Here you will find updated information about the changes being implemented for the grant scheme for Civil Society strengthening.
10 September 2026:
In autumn 2025, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs instructed Norad to change the grant scheme for strengthening civil society. In response to this instruction, Norad has collected new knowledge and made several clear recommendations. Based on this, the political leadership has decided on a new direction. Norad is now working to prepare these changes.
The restructuring will help to strengthen the role of local actors, ensure better results and make aid management more efficient.
&ndash; Norad must adapt to a new era. Going forward, we will place greater emphasis on genuine partnerships where money, power and influence are transferred to actors on the ground. Most people agree on this. Now we want to see it in practice, says Norad Director General Gunn Jorid Roset.
Greater competition among applicants is also an important consideration as the grant scheme for Civil Society Strengthening is being changed. This is the largest development assistance grant scheme targeting civil society, and many organisations naturally want updates and information about the changes to come.
&ndash; Both the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Norad have high ambitions, and many new elements must be developed. We do not yet have all the answers, but we promise to share relevant information with all interested parties as soon as we can, the Norad Director General continues.
Norad Director General Gunn Jorid Roset.
Photo: Fartein Rudjord/Norad
Norad is working along several tracks to prepare the restructuring of the grant scheme:
We are exploring adjusted objectives and new ways of measuring results
We are considering how the scheme can be geographically focused
We are considering changes in partnerships to ensure locally led development to a greater extent
We are planning calls for proposals that will communicate criteria, objectives and expectations to applicants in the best possible way
&ndash; We appreciate all the input we have received so far in connection with the restructuring of this scheme. We will seek further contributions from civil society organisations and other actors, both in Norway and in other countries, during the process, says Stine Horn, Director of the Department for Human Development in Norad.
Before decisions are made on the new design, Norad will openly invite all interested parties to comment on the proposals. This will take place in December 2026, after the Storting (Norwegian Parliament) has adopted the new national budget for 2027.
Call for proposals for Norwegian organisations in 2027 &ndash; call for proposals for international and South-based organisations in 2028
Norad aims to launch a call for proposals targeting Norwegian organisations in the first quarter of 2027, with funding decisions to be made late 2027 / early 2028. The application deadline has not yet been decided, but Norad will allow more time than usual so that local partners can play a central role in developing proposals. The timeline is also dependent on factors beyond Norad&rsquo;s control and will be updated on an ongoing basis.
&ndash; Although the 2027 call for proposals is limited to Norwegian organisations, we already want to see changes now. There will be greater requirements to ensure locally led development and equitable partnerships with actors in the Global South. Applicants must clearly demonstrate how they contribute to strengthening civil society in developing countries, says Stine Horn.
Norad is planning a more targeted call for proposals for South-based and international organisations in 2028, with agreements to be entered into in early 2029. Norad aims to gradually increase the share of direct support to South-based organisations, although support to Norwegian organisations working in partnership with local actors will still constitute the largest recipient group over this budget line.
Photo: Fartein Rudjord/Norad
Transitional arrangements
Organisations whose agreements expire before a potential new funding period will have their agreements extended. This means that Norwegian organisations with agreements expiring in 2026 or early in 2027 will have their agreements extended until the end of 2027.
All Norwegian organisations that wish to receive funding from Norad under this grant scheme in the period 2028&ndash;2032 may apply under the 2027 call for proposals, regardless of whether the organisation needs funding for the whole support period or only for the last four or three years of the period.
&ndash; It is important to emphasise that Norad does not plan to terminate any agreements. We will facilitate all existing agreements running until the end of their agreement period &ndash; with the usual reservation regarding appropriations by the Storting. This will ensure good transitional arrangements for those currently receiving funding, explains Horn.
Questions and comments regarding the process can be directed to menneskerettigheter.og.sivilt.samfunn@norad.no .
&ndash; We cannot promise to answer all questions as they arise, but we will share the information we can and ensure that we take all the input we receive into account, Horn concludes.
Civil Society
&laquo; Civil Society&raquo; is a dedicated grant scheme for civil society organisations, with the aim of strengthening civil society in developing countries. In 2025, the scheme provided approximately NOK 2.52 billion in funding.
Norad is currently restructuring the scheme. This involves: 1) sharpening the focus of the scheme on strengthening democratic development; 2) adjusting the objectives and introducing new ways of measuring results; 3) a stronger geographical focus; 4) changes to partnerships to further promote locally led development; and 5) changes to calls for proposals.
A call for proposals for Norwegian civil society organisations is planned for the first quarter of 2027, with implementation from 2028.
This will be followed by a more targeted call for proposals for international and Southern-based organisations in 2028.
