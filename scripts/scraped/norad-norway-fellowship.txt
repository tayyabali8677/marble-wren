Norad - The Norwegian Agency for Development Cooperation
Norad is an administrative and specialist body for development cooperation and supports the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the Ministry of Climate and the Environment in achieving the goals of Norwegian development policy and promoting sustainable development. The UN&#x27;s sustainability goals, climate goals and human rights form the basis of Norad&#x27;s work. Norad is based in Oslo and has around 320 employees.

Sudan: Looking to the future amid the crisis
During a highly challenging humanitarian situation, work on long-term measures continues. For many, it is about the possibility of looking ahead, even in the middle of the crisis. The Norwegian Refugee Council is helping to ensure that Hanaa and other women can gain greater control over their own lives.
Preventing the worst impacts of El Niño
El Niño is expected to cause widespread damage later this year. Norway is providing NOK 30 million to support early action that helps protect people from hunger and food insecurity.
Faster help when hunger crises strike
When drought, conflict and flooding threaten food supplies in vulnerable countries, rapid action is crucial. By supporting FAO&#x27;s emergency fund, SFERA, Norway is helping to protect food production, save lives and strengthen resilience. For Fardowsa in Somalia, this kind of support has made it possible to rebuild a livelihood after several years of drought.
Ebola in DR Congo: A crisis within a crisis
The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) is affecting a region already devastated by conflict, large numbers of internally displaced people and weak health systems. To help contain the outbreak, Norway is providing funding to Norwegian humanitarian organisations and major international emergency funds.
Trans people are losing access to life-saving healthcare
Global aid cuts are threatening health services and legal protection for trans people in several parts of the world. Norway is maintaining its support. That is helping to save lives.

The Nansen Support Programme for Ukraine
The Nansen Programme for Ukraine is a Norwegian support programme for both civilian and military aid, with a total budget of NOK 275 billion for the period 2023–2030.
Disturbing trend for humanitarian workers in Ukraine
– It has become more dangerous to be a humanitarian worker in Ukraine. Last week, two deminers from Norwegian People&#x27;s Aid were killed. This week, the Ukrainian Red Cross&#x27;s main warehouse in Kyiv was completely destroyed in a Russian attack, says Norad Director General Gunn Jorid Roset.
– Pride is not a celebration, it is an act of resistance
What is it like to stand up for fundamental human rights in a country under attack? Norad met two Ukrainian human rights defenders who, with support from Norway, are working to ensure that the rights of LGBTQ+ people are not put on hold during the war.
Ukrainian journalists expose corruption in wartime
Together with independent anti-corruption agencies and civil society, a free and independent press plays a central role in the fight against corruption.

Gaza: – Hard to comprehend how badly injured the children are
Red Cross doctor Roger Alcock has never seen so many injured people as in Gaza. At the field hospital in Rafah, they save lives and also create moments of joy for the children in the midst of the catastrophe.
Standing Together Against Illegal Gold Mining in the Amazon
In the heart of the Amazon, authorities and Indigenous peoples are joining forces in the fight against illegal gold mining.
The Rafto Prize Laureates from Sudan: As Long as We Live, We Believe in Democracy
In war-torn Sudan, local neighbourhood committees known as Emergency Response Rooms (ERR) are making an invaluable contribution to help their fellow citizens with water, food and health care. ERR brings hope when the world is at its darkest. Life is catastrophic for millions of people in Sudan.
They support each other in the fight against corruption
«Why shouldn’t we have our own global network when the criminals have theirs?», said Eva Joly when she launched the international Corruption Hunter Network in 2005 – a network led and supported by Norad.
Historic agreement for women shea workers in Ghana
Hardworking and underpaid women in Ghana are now seeing their labour rights strengthened. The country’s first collective agreement in the shea sector has been signed, with support from Norway.
The world’s most important backup: seeds for the future
Deep in the permafrost of Svalbard, more than one million seed samples are stored – a global insurance policy in case vital plant species disappear. At the same time, farmers in Somalia are testing new seed varieties to strengthen food production in the face of climate change and conflict.

This section on Norad&rsquo;s webpages provides potential applicants for funding from Norad and existing grant recipients with an overview of the main expectations as well as legal and financial rules applicable to project/programme grants from Norad. These pages are relevant for civil society organisations and other private actors such as research institutes and private sector entities.
Note that the information on this webpage is not exhaustive and applicants are therefore asked to carefully review any documentation shared by Norad, specifically grant agreements, and direct communication with Norad staff. The grant agreement constitutes the legal basis for a grant. This text in these Guidelines is not legally binding.

Grant scheme rules are a set of rules describing how the Norwegian Govenment's National Budget (Prop 1 S) should be operationalised in connection with allocation of funds to grant recipients. Calls for Proposals by Norad hence have to be aligned with the grant scheme rules for the relevant budget post. Norad is responsible for drafting and revising the grant scheme rules for budget posts that have been either fully or partially delegated from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs to Norad. The Ministry approved them.

Faster help when hunger crises strike
Published 7/13/2026

Updated 7/13/2026

Bildetekst
Photo: Finbarr O&#x27;Reilly/The New York/NTB
A farmer prepares to plant onions on a cooperative farm in Dollow, Somalia, April 2026. The cost of diesel to run the tractor has more than doubled.
Photo: Finbarr O&#x27;Reilly/The New York/NTB
When drought, conflict and flooding threaten food supplies in vulnerable countries, rapid action is crucial. By supporting FAO&#x27;s emergency fund, SFERA, Norway is helping to protect food production, save lives and strengthen resilience. For Fardowsa in Somalia, this kind of support has made it possible to rebuild a livelihood after several years of drought.
When drought struck Somalia, Fardowsa lost much of what she relied on to support her family. As a single parent, she experienced how years of drought had gradually eroded her finances and made everyday life increasingly precarious.
&ndash; Support that arrives early can make all the difference in whether families are able to get through a crisis and rebuild their income, says Martin Samuelsen, Head of Section for Humanitarian Efforts at Norad.
For Fardowsa, the support meant a fresh start. Through the UN&#x27;s Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), she received help paying off debt, buying essential goods and rebuilding a small café.
&ndash; Now I can provide for my children and plan for the future, she says.
For millions of people in similar situations, this kind of support is about preventing acute crises from turning into prolonged hunger and poverty.
When food crises threaten future food production
In many humanitarian crises, the response isn't only about providing food here and now. Just as important is protecting people's ability to produce food in the future.
When farmers lose their seed stocks, or livestock die as a result of drought and disease, the consequences can last for years. That's why rapid support to safeguard agricultural production and food security is a vital part of the humanitarian response. In many cases, support for food production can reduce the need for large-scale food distribution later on.
&ndash; Once food production collapses, it takes a long time to rebuild. That's why it's essential to act early to protect both crops and livestock, says Samuelsen.
This is precisely where FAO plays a key role &ndash; by helping people maintain food production during and after crises.
Early action and rapid response to drought help protect both people and livestock. In Somalia, different types of livestock often graze together, allowing herders to make the best use of available vegetation. Here, veterinarians examine the animals and speak with their owners to assess the animals&#x27; health and treatment needs in Qansax-Dheere, Bay, May 2026.
Photo: Hassan Shurbe/Arete/FAO
Norway's contribution to rapid and flexible crisis support
Through its support for FAO's emergency fund SFERA, Norway is helping ensure that assistance can be deployed quickly when needs arise &ndash; without having to wait for new funding decisions.
&ndash; Norway's humanitarian funding is flexible and predictable, which enables our partners to respond quickly when crises occur, says Samuelsen.
Since 2022, Norwegian contributions have been part of the response in 22 countries, reaching more than 3 million people affected by conflict, climate-related disasters and other crises.
Support has included:
cash transfers to vulnerable families
distribution of seeds and agricultural inputs
veterinary services and animal feed
measures to protect local communities from future crises
Acting before a crisis worsens
A key part of SFERA is so-called anticipatory action &ndash; measures put in place before a forecast weather event hits with full force. The aim is to prevent the event from developing into a humanitarian disaster.
In Cameroon, FAO has used early flood warnings to provide support to vulnerable families in advance. Cash assistance and preventive measures enabled households to secure food, protect assets and prepare more effectively.
Such early action can reduce both human suffering and the need for far more costly relief efforts later on.
When we manage to act before a crisis hits, we can significantly reduce the scale of the damage and give people a better chance of getting through difficult periods.
Martin Samuelsen
Head of Section for Humanitarian Efforts at Norad
For Fardowsa, the support meant the chance to establish a new source of income after several years of drought.
For millions of other people in vulnerable areas, early and flexible funding is about protecting food production, income and future prospects before a crisis develops into hunger and prolonged suffering.
Ali Adan/Arete/FAO
SFERA (Special Fund for Emergency and Rehabilitation Activities) is the Food and Agriculture Organization's flexible humanitarian fund for rapid response to food crises.
The fund provides early and flexible financing that can be used immediately in a crisis to protect and restore people's access to food. Funds can be used for seeds, veterinary services, repairs to critical infrastructure, and other measures that reduce the impact of drought, flooding and other disasters.
Early action helps reduce both human suffering and overall humanitarian costs.
Read more about SFERA on FAO's website
Humanitarian and comprehensive response
Eyad Baba/AFP/NTB
Norwegian humanitarian assistance is the support Norway provides to people affected by war, crises and natural disasters.
The aim is to save lives, alleviate suffering and safeguard human dignity and rights in situations of crisis and conflict. Humanitarian assistance includes emergency relief, as well as other forms of crisis response.
We have a duty to assist people in need, regardless of who they are or where they live.
