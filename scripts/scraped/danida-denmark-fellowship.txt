Danida Fellowship Centre supports Denmark's development cooperation by strengthening knowledge and capacity in partner countries.
With funding from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Denmark, we offer learning activities, support development research, foster university partnerships, and promote local and international knowledge sharing. Participation is limited to partners in Danida financed projects and programmes and professionals from eligible institutions who meet the application requirements of our programmes.
WHAT WE OFFER
Learning
We design, manage, and deliver learning activities aimed at strengthening individual competencies, enhancing institutional capacity, and supporting organisational change in countries that partner with Denmark through Danida partner countries.
Read more
WHAT WE OFFER
Research
We administer grants to development research, support collaborative research partnerships that generate new knowledge, strengthen academic capacity, and inform policy and practice – all with the goal of advancing sustainable development in Danida partner countries.
Read more
WHAT WE OFFER
University partnerships
The two major university cooperation programmes, the Knowledge and Innovation Programme and Building Stronger Universities, managed by Danida Fellowship Centre focus on strengthening partnerships, capacity building, networks, and collaborative lerning in higher education an universities

WHAT WE OFFER
Network
The very minute you are accepted as a Danida Fellow, you automatically become part of the Danida Fellows Network. The network is open to all current and former participants in learning activities administered by the Danida Fellowship Centre, as well as to Master&#8217;s students in Denmark funded by Danida and researchers involved in Danida-supported research projects.

Free online course for Danida fellows. Join “Learn - Apply – Act” - Danida Fellowship Centre
Free online course for Danida fellows. Join “Learn - Apply – Act”
Published
September 9, 2026
Have you completed a course or learning activity through Danida Fellowship Centre? Now is your opportunity to build on what you have learned and turn your knowledge and ideas into action! Submit your application before the deadline on 17 September 2026 to secure your spot for the course .
Through interactive workshops, coaching and peer learning, you will gain practical tools to:
Create positive change in your workplace or organisation
Turn ideas and knowledge into concrete action
Strengthen your skills in organisational change,
communication and design thinking
Develop and test an action plan based on a real challenge
from your work
Watch the short presentation video of the course here.
Ready to take the next step as a change-maker?
Apply now!
WHO CAN APPLY?
Danida fellows from DFC’s learning courses or PhD students.
You must be available and committed to participate actively in one pre-course kick-off session , in all three online workshops in either the Morning or Evening CEST sessions, and in minimum one coaching session . It is also a requirement that you are committed and able to spend the needed time in between workshops to work on independent tasks and assignments.
WHEN TO APPLY?
Deadline for submission of application is 17 September 2026.
HOW TO APPLY?
Go to DFC’s application site: https://dfcentre.smapply.io/
Create a user profile and when asked about what you are applying for, select “Opportunities for Danida Fellows and Alumni”.
If you have a user profile already, go to your account, choose the Eligibility tab and click “Update your eligibility profile” and select “Opportunities for Danida Fellows and Alumni”.
Choose the programme:Learn –Apply –Act (October –December 2026); Enabling Change Agency for the transfer of knowledge into action -DFC Scholarship Programme.
Fill out your application, get a recommendation from your supervisor, and submitbefore the deadline 17 September 2026.
There is space for a maximum total of 100 participants, and prioritisation will be given to alumni of 2025 and 2026.
If you have any questions about this opportunity, then please reach us at nextstepnetworking@dfcentre.dk

Annual report 2025 for  Knowledge and Innovation Programme
In its first year, the Knowledge and Innovation Programme (KIP) launched with a DKK 430 million budget for 2025-2032, aiming to strengthen Africa-Denmark ties through university partnerships and student scholarships.
From 42 applications received after the June 2025 call, 10 projects were granted, five partnership and five seed funding projects, spanning five Danish universities and partners across nine African countries.

How to operate a wastewater treatment plant - Danida Fellowship Centre
How to operate a wastewater treatment plant
Published
September 9, 2026
How do you build the skills needed to operate a wastewater treatment plant – and make sure that the knowledge reaches beyond the people who take part in the initial training programme?
This was the focus of the Operation of Wastewater Treatment Plants course, organised by Danida Fellowship Centre in collaboration with Ferskvandscentret Silkeborg, Danish water utilities and technical consultants.
The programme brought together 25 participants from South Africa, Kenya, Ghana, Ukraine and Colombia. They came with different professional backgrounds and, collectively, more than 200 years of experience working with water and wastewater. The training combined technical capacity building with a Training of Trainers (ToT) approach, with the aim of enabling participants to share their knowledge when they returned to their workplaces.
An important part of the course was the technical training led by experienced professionals from the Danish water sector. Participants worked with both theoretical and practical aspects of wastewater treatment and contributed their own experiences from different contexts.
Read more on LinkedIn about the start of the training
A central part of the programme was the opportunity to see wastewater treatment in practice. Participants visited and spent time at Danish wastewater treatment plants, working alongside Danish colleagues and getting first-hand insight into how the plants were operated.
The internships took place at
Aarhus Vand – Viby and Egå wastewater treatment plants
BIOFOS – Lynetten and Avedøre wastewater treatment plants
VandCenter Syd – Ejby Mølle wastewater treatment plant
These visits gave participants an opportunity to connect the technical training with daily operations and to exchange experience with colleagues working in the Danish water sector.
See more on LinkedIn from the training and site visits
The second equally important part of the programme focused on what would happen after the participants returned home. Through the Training of Trainers component, participants worked with tools for developing and delivering training, as well as project and change management. They also developed individual action plans for how they would use and share their new knowledge in their own organisations.
The aim was therefore not only to strengthen the skills of the 25 participants, but also to enable them to pass on what they had learned to colleagues and contribute to change in their workplaces.
After 13 intensive days, the Denmark-based part of the programme was completed on 14 August. Participants returned home with new technical knowledge, practical experience from Danish wastewater treatment plants and plans for how to put their learning into use.

The first year of the Knowledge and Innovation Programme - Danida Fellowship Centre
The first year of the Knowledge and Innovation Programme
Published
June 10, 2026
22 applications were received, and five projects were selected in the first round of the new large-scale university partnership programme, Knowledge and Innovation.
With a budget of DKK 430 million between 2025-32, the programme strengthens partnerships between Danish and African universities and is a central element of Denmark’s Africa’s Century strategy , focusing on engagement through innovation and education. Its main objective is to promote university collaboration and student exchange between Denmark and African countries. By supporting university partnerships and providing scholarships for African students to study in Denmark, the programme fosters knowledge sharing and co-creation of solutions to global challenges.
The programme was launched by the Danish Ministry of Higher Education and Science and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs at the dialogue festival Folkemødet on Bornholm in June 2025.
As Minister for Higher Education and Science, Christina Egelund, said in the press release:
I look forward to welcoming more African students to study at Danish higher education institutions, and I hope that more Danish students will also seek exchange opportunities in African countries. Africa has immense potential, which we can help unleash through education.
The interest in the programme has been substantial. Over 250 participants from both Danish and African universities joined a Danida Fellowship Centre hosted online information session about the partnership programme. Following the application round, five partnerships were selected for support and funding by the Selection Committee for the Knowledge and Innovation Programme . In addition to the selected partnerships, the programme provides seed funding for pilot projects. Five projects received seed funding, and are expected to apply for a full partnership in 2026, alongside other applicants.
Early experience highlights that successful partnerships require:
Early involvement of all partners
Use of existing faculty and institutional resources
Flexibility to navigate different cultures, priorities, and administrative processes

Functional
4
Functional cookies enable a website to provide enhanced functionality and personalization. These cookies remember user preferences and choices, such as login details, language settings, or region selection, ensuring a more seamless and customized browsing experience. Without functional cookies, certain features of the website may not work as intended, leading to a less optimized user experience.
Cookies are small text files that are stored on your computer or device when you visit a website. These files contain various pieces of information that websites use to enhance your user experience.
Cookies are used to gather data about your behavior on the website. This information may include the number of visits, which pages you've visited, how much time you've spent on each page, and more. This data helps website owners understand how their site is being used and enables them to customize content and advertising to match your interests.
It's important to note that cookies are not inherently harmful and cannot contain viruses or malware. They are simply text files used to store information. However, it's crucial to be aware that certain cookies may contain personal information, which can be sensitive. Therefore, rules and laws have been implemented to require websites to inform users about the types of cookies they use and obtain consent before placing cookies on users' devices.
