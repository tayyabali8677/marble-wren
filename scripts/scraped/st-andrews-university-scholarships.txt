Scholarships and bursaries
St Andrews is committed to attracting the very best students, regardless of financial circumstances. The University offers a wide range of scholarships, bursaries and other funding opportunities to support applicants and students. T hese types of financial aid for education may be awarded based on financial need, academic excellence or other achievements, such as leadership, sport or music, and do not need to be repaid.
Our portfolio includes bursaries to reduce accommodation costs, international scholarships covering full or partial tuition fees, and funding for students from the UK to help supplement government sources and alleviate the financial burden of basic living costs. Applicants with refugee status, humanitarian protection, discretionary leave and asylum seeker status are encouraged to apply for financial support scholarships.
The scholarships available to you will depend on factors such as your chosen degree programme, where you currently live, your household income, your academic achievements, and your fee status.

Calculating household income for scholarship applications
Complete the financial funding tool and calculate household income
Use this guide to complete the financial funding tool and prepare the correct evidence when applying for means-tested scholarships based on financial need .
Current students seeking in-year funding should check individual scholarship pages or contact the Money Advice team by emailing moneyadvice@st-andrews.ac.uk .
After submitting your course application, you will be emailed login details for the My Application portal within two working days. You must have these details before you can apply for scholarships.
If you are applying for a scholarship based on financial need, complete the financial funding tool first. This will automatically populate the "annual household income" section of all your scholarship applications. You only need to complete it once unless your circumstances change.
How to complete the financial funding tool
Select either young student or independent student .
If you are a postgraduate student , you will usually select independent student.
Your status determines whose income you must provide.
Young students
Meet all of the following:
Under 25 before your course start date
Live in the family home and are financially dependent on parents or guardians
Have no dependent children
Have not supported yourself through work or benefits for 3 years
Are not married, in a civil partnership or living with a partner
Independent students
Meet any of the following:
Are 25 or over on your course start date
Have supported yourself through work or benefits for 3 years
Have a dependent child living with you
Have no living parents
Were married, in a civil partnership or living with a partner before your course start date
Have not communicated with either of your biological or adoptive parents for 12 months before your course begins and the situation is irreconcilable
Have care experience or custodial care
How to calculate household income
Your household income is the gross annual income of the people financially responsible for you.
Young students
Provide the combined income of:
Your parents or guardians
Their partner or spouse, if applicable
Independent students
Provide the combined income of:
Yourself
Your spouse or civil partner, or any partner you live with, whether you are married or not
What counts as income and what evidence to upload
Income type
What we count
Evidence required
Salary or earnings
Total gross income (before tax), including bonuses or overtime
P60, final payslip, Annual Tax Summary
Self-employment, business, trusts, dividends, investments
Taxable profit
Self-Assessment return, accountant statement, investment statements
Disability allowances or income-replacement benefits
Any benefit replacing earnings
DWP or government letters showing full amount or 3 months of statements
Student loans, bursaries, maintenance
Maintenance only (not tuition payments)
SAAS or SFE or equivalent award letters
Private or state pensions
Total pension income
Annual pension statement
Care experience
Confirmation of care status
Local authority letter
Asylum seeker with no income
Confirmation of status
Home Office documentation
If you cannot provide the above, you may upload three months of bank statements with income clearly highlighted and explained.
All documents should be in PDF or similar small file format and in English .
Benefits to include
Provide evidence for the following. If a benefit is not listed here, you do not need to include it:
Carer&rsquo;s Allowance or Carer Support Payment
Employment and Support Allowance
Income Support
Incapacity Benefit
Jobseeker's Allowance
Maternity Allowance
Pension Credit
State Retirement Pension
Universal Credit (standard allowance only)
Working Tax Credits (not Child Tax Credits)
War Widow or War Disablement pensions
Industrial Injuries benefits
Other income-replacement benefits listed by SAAS

Stipend rate for postgraduate research students
University of St Andrews stipend rate
The University of St Andrews stipend rate applies to postgraduate research stipends that are fully funded and administered by the University.
For the 2025-2026 academic year , the stipend rate is &pound;19,775 .
For the 2026-2027 academic year , the stipend rate will increase to &pound;20,467 , representing a 3.5% rise on the previous year. This rate will apply to stipend payments issued from Tuesday 1 September 2026 .
The stipend rate is reviewed annually, and details of any change for the 2027-2028 academic year will be confirmed by the end of December 2026 .
How the stipend rate is determined
The stipend rate is reviewed each year by the University PhD Stipend Rate Review Group, established in 2026. The group considers a range of evidence and makes recommendations to the Scholarships Board, chaired by the Principal, which approves the final rate.
Membership of the Review Group includes student representation as well as colleagues from Postgraduate Research, Finance, Registry, Student Services, and Student Experience. Student input is gathered through the Postgraduate Research Representative Forum and through feedback provided by Schools.
Approach to setting the 2026-2027 rate
In determining the 2026-2027 stipend rate, the Review Group considered:
feedback from postgraduate research students
inflation data, including the Consumer Price Index (CPI)
wider cost-of-living factors
sector benchmarking and external stipend rates
the University&rsquo;s financial position
The approved rate of &pound;20,467 reflects a balance between financial sustainability and the University&rsquo;s commitment to supporting a diverse and active research community. The 3.5% increase exceeds the rate of inflation at the time of the decision (2.8% in the 12 months to April 2026).
Stipends are scholarships rather than salaries and are therefore not subject to taxation. The University also recognises that postgraduate research funding is diverse, with many students fully or partially self-funded and competing for available funding opportunities.
Eligibility and confirmation
For new entrants, whether the University of St Andrews stipend rate applies will be stated clearly in:
scholarship advertisements
scholarship award letters
Current students who receive this rate will have been notified directly by their School or the Scholarships Team. If you are unsure which stipend rate applies to you, you should contact the Scholarships Team at ser-fund@st-andrews.ac.uk .
Additional financial support
There may be other scholarships and bursaries available to postgraduate research students. Further information can be found in the University&rsquo;s scholarships catalogue .
The Money Team in Student Services can assist students seeking advice on financial topics from budgeting and saving to benefits.  The Money Team also administers several funds available to students facing unexpected financial difficulties, including discretionary funding and loans to help with temporary cash-flow problems. More information can be found on the Financial Support and Money Advice webapge .
To ask a question or request an appointment please complete this  short online enquiry form .  You can also find money advice and tips on the St Andrews Student Life Blog .
If you are a parent or carer, you can consult our guides for students with caring responsibilities .
