Funding lines
NWO harmonises the current funding instruments as much as possible to facilitate collaboration. As a result, researchers, irrespective of the research domain, will be subject to the same conditions as much as possible. Harmonisation will facilitate both applicants and NWO to work more across the boundaries of disciplines and domains.
NWO will provide a limited palette of funding instruments with a clear number of modules. These modules can be combined in accordance with the objectives of the programme or call concerned. This approach will provide the flexibility needed to meet the needs of the various disciplines.
Objective each funding line
The harmonisation of instruments will provide the following palette of funding lines and each line will have a distinct objective:
Open Competition
Funding of curiosity-driven research in the four scientific domains, thus enabling researchers to conduct research into a subject of their own choice without being bound by thematic conditions.
Choose one of the following domains:
Science
Social Sciences and Humanities (SSH)
Applied and Engineering Sciences (AES)
ZonMw (Health research)
Talent Programme
Personal funding for individual researchers, whether working in teams or otherwise. For example, we finance curiosity-driven research aligned to the various stages in the careers of researchers through the Talent Programme.
NWO Talent programme Veni | Vidi | Vici
Rubicon
Collaboration with knowledge users and society
Projects or programmes for thematic research and valorisation in partnership with external public and/or private parties. These projects or programmes are designed to increase and accelerate the economic or social impact of the research. Examples are programmes or projects falling under the auspices of the National Research Agenda, the Knowledge and Innovation Covenant (focusing on major challenges facing society which require more knowledge and ground-breaking innovations) and the National Growth Fund (large-scale investments to promote long-term economic growth). The international and European thematic calls also fall under this funding line.
KIC (Knowledge and Innovation Covenant)
NWA (Dutch Research Agenda)
NGF (National Growth Fund)
International programme
Practice-oriented research
Investment in the professionalisation, quality improvement and self-organisation of practice-oriented research in the universities of applied sciences. This funding is provided using different instruments managed by the Taskforce for Applied Research SIA .
Infrastructure
We are committed to a balanced and future-proof ecosystem of high-quality scientific infrastructure for all fields of science. This includes highly specialised equipment, data collections and digital infrastructure. Access, innovation and continuity are always key. Read more
Innovation Accelerator
We accelerate the path from research results to applications in society. We create space for the development and further development of innovative services and products based on knowledge in a wide range of sectors. We do this through a range of different options, such as grants, training courses and loans, tailored to the needs of researchers. Read more
Modules
In the majority of cases, the programmes and projects from the aforementioned funding lines will be built up from the following modules:
Personnel
Researcher in training/PhD, postdoc, researcher, non-scientific personnel
Materials
Investments: infrastructure and data files
Knowledge utilisation
Internationalisation
Citizen Science

Applying for funding, how does it work?
Applying for funding, how does it work?
Researchers and research institutions can submit an application for funding for research projects, large equipment and databases as soon as NWO publishes a call for project proposals. NWO has a fixed procedure for handling all applications. How the application procedure is set up – from the moment you apply until a decision is reached – is detailed below.
Application procedure
The call for proposals describes the aim of a grant round, who can apply, the amount of money available, which criteria your application needs to meet, how and when you can submit your application, and when, at the latest, NWO will reach a decision regarding your application.
NWO generally allocates research grants through a tender procedure. Applications that were submitted during a given grant round are assessed and subsequently ranked according to predetermined criteria. The best applications end up highest in the rankings and are the first to be eligible for a grant. The grants are allocated according to these rankings, until the limit of the grant budget has been reached.
Application
Qualification system
NWO gives all full proposals a qualification based on the applicable criteria. Such a qualification offers applicants and applying universities a uniform insight into the quality evaluation of the research proposal. And it offers NWO a good overview of the quality of the applications and enables NWO to see how many excellent or very good proposals are submitted
The qualification will be made known to the researcher in the same letter in which he or she is also informed about NWO's decision whether or not to award funding.
The qualification has the following categories:
excellent
very good
good
unsatisfactory
The qualification system applies to almost all funding instruments from NWO. If not applicable, this is mentioned in the call for proposals of that specific round.
Submitting your application
You can submit an application after a call for proposals has been published. In some grant rounds, we first ask you to submit a letter of intent and/or a pre-proposal (shorter application) or an initiative. Always use the form on the funding page of the grant round in question when drafting your application.
Code for Dealing with Personal Interests
For a fair and consistent application procedure, it is essential that your application is assessed without any (semblance of) bias. To ensure this, NWO applies the Code for Dealing with Personal Interests. This applies to all persons involved in your application’s assessment and decision-making process.
Code for Dealing with Personal Interests
Application system
You submit your application through ISAAC, NWO’s online application system. Only the main applicant can submit an application. You need an account for this, which you can create yourself. As soon as we have received your application, we will send you a confirmation of receipt.
We advise you to start submitting your application at least one day before the deadline. The reason for this is that you will need to enter information online as well.
If you have any technical questions about ISAAC, please consult the online manual. If you cannot find the answer to your question there, then contact:
ISAAC helpdesk | telephone number: +31 70 3440600 | email: isaac.helpdesk@nwo.nl
ISAAC manual
NWO research fields
For all applications it is compulsory to fill out the main research field that corresponds to the subject of your research proposal.
NWO research fields
Salary tables
Do you need information about personnel costs for your proposal? Consult the collective labour agreements of the academic medical centres (NFU) and of the universities (Universities of the Netherlands).
Salary tables
Authorisation scheme for project managers
Project managers can authorise others to carry out administrative actions for their project in ISAAC, NWO’s application and reporting system.
Authorisation scheme for project managers
Check whether we can process your application
After you have submitted your application, NWO will let you know whether it is processing your application as soon as possible. For this to happen, your application must meet the conditions described in the call for proposals. Among other things, we will check whether your application has been submitted in the prescribed language, whether you have made use of the available application form, whether your application was submitted via ISAAC, and whether we received it before the deadline. If we find that this is not the case, we will contact you. You will then be given a one-off opportunity to rectify this (if possible). We will also contact you if we do process your application.
Application procedure Regieorgaan SIA, or Taskforce for Applied Research SIA differs
Are you applying for a grant from Taskforce for Applied Research SIA, or Regieorgaan SIA? If so, the application and assessment procedure differs in a number of ways. For more information, please visit the website.
Funding Regieorgaan SIA (only in Dutch)
Assessment of your application
Pre-selection
If NWO receives a large number of applications, NWO may decide to hold a pre-selection. This may occur if the total amount of the grant applications is four times the total available budget. In the event of a pre-selection, the selection committee will make an overall assessment of the applications based on the assessment criteria that apply to the round in question. The committee will give you the opportunity to submit a rebuttal, after which it will advise NWO to reject the less promising applications.
Peer review by external referees
Before the selection committee considers your application, NWO will first seek input from at least two external referees. These are independent advisors who are experts in the subject area of your application. They will assess your application based on the assessment criteria outlined in the call for proposals. They are generally given three to four weeks to do this.
A referee will assess no more than one application per grant round. As a matter of principle, referees are not paid for their assessments. In the case of a multidisciplinary research proposal, NWO tries to select referees from the different disciplines who are also familiar with comparable multidisciplinary research.
NWO asks referees to treat research proposals confidentially to protect the privacy of the applicants as well as any intellectual property rights. For its part, NWO treats the identity of referees confidentially. Their identity is not disclosed to either the selection committee or the applicants. NWO therefore guarantees that referees are able to give an open opinion on the research proposals they are assessing.
Rebuttal to referee reports
As soon as NWO has received the referee reports, we will forward them to you. You will subsequently be given several business days to respond in writing (the ‘rebuttal’). You can answer referees’ questions and address any criticisms in your rebuttal.
Interview or site visit (optional)
An interview or site visit is part of the assessment procedure in some grant rounds. The purpose of an interview is among others to give the selection committee an impression of your managerial skills, the research skills and the persuasiveness of the researcher(s). During an interview, you will be asked to give a short presentation or introduction. Subsequently, the members of the committee will ask questions about your research proposal.
A site visit can take place for an application for the development of extensive research facilities. Some or all of the selection committee will visit the location where the research facility to be subsidised is located or will be located. This makes it possible to assess whether the proper development of the facility can be guaranteed.
Assessment by the selection committee
The selection committee issues advice on which applications should be granted. This is done based on the written material: the research proposal, the referee reports and your rebuttal. An interview or site visit can be part of the assessment.
The members of the selection committee award applications a score for each criterion on a scale of 1 to 9, based on which the final qualification is established.
In principle, NWO applies normalisation to committee members' scores in order to produce a ranking for all funding instruments. Normalisation is used because committee members interpret and use the score scale from 1 to 9 differently. NWO adopts a normalisation approach based on the median as measure of central tendency and the average deviation relative to the median as measure for the spread of scores.
Based on the prioritisation overview of the normalised (pre-)scores, the selection committee has a substantive discussion about this initial ranking. At the end of this discussion, the committee members jointly determine a substantively motivated final score and jointly decide on the final ranking.
Depending on the type of subsidy instrument, this recommendation is sent to the relevant domain board or the board of directors for final approval. The subsidies are then normally awarded in order of this ranking until the available subsidy budget has been exhausted.
NWO has the authority not to award the grants (in full) according to their ranking, on policy grounds (for example, a balanced distribution across themes). If these policy grounds apply, then they will be stated in the call for proposals.
Join a selection committee/jury yourself?
Decision on your application
Letter of decision
After having assessed whether the selection committee’s advice was reached in a consistent manner, NWO will either grant or reject your application. You can see which NWO governing body is responsible for deciding on your application in the call for proposals.
As soon as NWO has issued a decision on your application, you will receive a letter of decision from NWO. This letter also contains the details of the selection committee’s assessment.
Lodging an objection
If you do not agree with the decision on your application, you may lodge an objection. How to lodge an objection is described at the bottom of the decision letter.
Lodging an objection
After being awarded a grant: starting your project
The grants awarded by the NWO are subject to certain conditions. For example, NWO ensures that the grant money is spent correctly. The grant decision you receive from NWO specifies which documents we need from you before the project can be launched. Read more about starting your project.

Laws and regulations concerning funding
As a funder of research, NWO manages a substantial budget of public funds. It is therefore important that NWO has the confidence of the relevant stakeholders that it is fulfilling its statutory task with expertise and integrity. This is guaranteed by establishing competences and responsibility, and developing clear rules and procedures. Here, you can consult all legislation, policy rules and codes of conduct that specify NWO’s tasks and responsibilities.
Legal base
As an independent directive body (founded in 1950) with the authority to distribute public resources, NWO falls under the responsibility of the Ministry of Education, Culture and Science. The tasks and responsibilities are established in the NWO Act.

Seven grants awarded to explore natural biochemical diversity for more sustainable production processes
Seven grants awarded to explore natural biochemical diversity for more sustainable production processes
17 September 2026
Shutterstock
NWO has awarded funding to seven projects under the KIC call ‘Biochemical Diversity’. The consortia, comprising public and private partners, will receive a total of 8 million euros to harness biochemical substances and pathways from nature for the development of more sustainable production processes.
Synthetic chemicals and man-made production processes can have negative side effects, such as pollution or the depletion of raw materials. Nature offers an immense variety of biochemical substances and pathways that can make current polluting production processes more environmentally friendly and efficient. That is why NWO and the Knowledge and Innovation Agenda’s (KIA) KIA Key Enabling Technologies and KIA Agriculture, Water and Food are investing in fundamental changes to production processes in order to preserve the liveability of our planet.
From crop protection to the development of improved medicines: the projects focus on developing and refining key enabling technologies that enable the exploration, analysis and application of the natural biochemical diversity.
To further increase the impact and make room for an additional highly rated proposal, NWO has decided to award an extra project within this round. To this end, a one-off sum of 1 million euros has been added to the available budget, drawn from the 2026 KIC budget.
The awarded projects
Spatial Profiling of Interactions and Chemical Ecological diversity for sustainable agriculture (SPICE) - dr. Francisco Barona Gomez (Universiteit Leiden)
Co-applicants: prof. J.J.J. van der Hooft (WUR), prof. dr. A.T. Kovacs (UL), dr. P.W. Lindenburg (HSLeiden)
Co-funders: Koppert BV, Endogenomiks, Waters, Artemis Natuurlijk, CTGB, Bionomic
Agriculture is shifting globally from chemical-dependent toward more sustainable production. Yet crop protection remains essential. Microorganisms, 1.e., Biological Control Agents (BCAs), can provide this through bioactive compounds called metabolites. Much about these compounds remains unknown, limiting large-scale application. SPICE aims to close this knowledge gap using DESI-MS, an advanced technique that measures molecules directly on living samples, revealing what BCAs produce and how they work. SPICE connects universities, a university of applied sciences, a regulatory authority, a branch organization, and industry partners. The knowledge generated will advance science, applications, and education in service of safe, effective biological crop protection.
Discovery of biocatalysts by structure-based genome mining (BIOGEM) – prof. dr. ir. Marco W. Fraaije (Rijksuniversiteit Groningen)
Co-applicants: dr. M.J.L.J. Fürst (RUG), dr. ir. M.A. Kabel (WUR)
Co-funders: Novonesis, GECCO biotech
This project aims to discover new enzymes, nature’s tiny chemical machines, using advanced computer tools. Instead of relying only on known examples, the BIOGEM project will develop a new way to scan genetic information and identify enzymes that can carry out difficult and valuable chemical reactions. The project will focus on enzymes that need helper molecules (cofactors) to function, as these can perform powerful transformations. In addition, BIOGEM will improve methods to produce these enzymes efficiently. By combining expertise from universities and biotechnology companies, the project seeks to unlock nature’s hidden chemical potential (biochemical diversity) for greener and more sustainable technologies.
Soil Chemical Ecology for SustaiNable Crop ProtecTion (SCENT) - prof. dr. Paolina V. Garbeva (KNAW)
Co-applicants: Dr. dipl.-ing. V. Cordovez da Cunha (KNAW), Prof. dr. dipl.-ing J. Dickschat (KNAW, Uni Bonn), prof. dr. M.H. Medema (WUR), prof. dr. J.M. Raaijmakers (KNAW), Prof. dr. dipl.-ing G.P. van Wezel (UL)
Co-funders: Agriodor, Isobionics, Bejo Zaden B.V., BioClearEarth, Joordens Zaden
SCENT (Soil Chemical Ecology for SustaiNable Crop ProtecTion) unlocks the largely untapped biochemical diversity of microbial volatile compounds produced by soil bacteria to develop key enabling technologies for sustainable crop protection. By integrating volatilomics, genomics, bioinformatics and synthetic biology, SCENT identifies, predicts and optimises pathogen-suppressing volatiles. The project translates this knowledge into steering strategies and a publicly accessible database, reducing reliance on synthetic pesticides and strengthening resilient, sustainable food production systems.
TERpenoid Reconstruction And Pathway Annotation Through multi-omics and Heterologous production (TERRAPATH) – dr. Justin J.J. van der Hooft (Wageningen University and Research)
Co-applicants: dr. L. Asveld (TU Delft), prof. dr. ir. J.G. Daran (TU Delft), prof. dr. M.H. Medema (WUR), prof. dr. J.T. Pronk (TU Delft)
Co-funders: dsm-firmenich, DAB.bio
Collaborating partners: University of Bonn, John Innes Centre, Biomia & TU Denmark, Heineken, Johannes Gutenberg University Mainz, University of Strasbourg, Fujian Agriculture and Forestry University
Terpenoids are valuable natural ingredients in flavours and fragrances, providing alternatives to synthetic molecules. Yet, most terpene biochemical diversity remains unexplored. By integrating multi-omics analysis, AI, yeast biotechnology, and prioritization using societal considerations, the researchers will discover and produce novel terpenoids, thus expanding the repertoire of safe sustainable bio-based ingredients.
Neutral, Low Energy & Re-usable Cellulases Using Waste Stream Carbon (MILDCELL) – prof. dr. Arthur F.J. Ram (Universiteit Leiden)
Co-applicant: dr. J. Jiang (UT)
Co-funders: Recell Group B.V., Lamoral Holding B.V. c.s.
Collaboration partner: BioSolum B.V.
MILDCELL develops a new generation of energy‑efficient enzymes that can break down cellulose under mild, low‑temperature conditions. These enzymes are produced by fungi that will grow on industrial glycol‑rich waste streams, turning low‑value residues into valuable resources. The project demonstrates the technology in two applications: converting wastewater‑recovered cellulose and enabling more sustainable, low‑temperature textile processing. By creating reusable enzyme systems and circular production routes, MILDCELL reduces energy use, chemical consumption, and environmental impact. The results offer affordable and scalable technology with potential benefits for multiple industries.
Translating Protein Diversity into Next-Generation Biotherapeutics - prof. dr. Anouk M. Rijs (VU Amsterdam)
Co-applicants: dr. M.H.A. Bos (LUMC), prof. dr. G.W. Somsen (VU)
Co-funders: VarmX, VectorY, Thermo Fisher Scientific, CMP Scientific, MS Vision, TOSOH Bioscience
Collaborating partners: LUMC, AUMC
The NextBio public-private consortium aims to improve medical treatments by better understanding the many different forms a single protein can take, known as proteoforms. Because protein function and drug effectiveness often depend on specific proteoforms, NextBio develops advanced analytical tools to study them accurately. Bringing together experts from academia, biotech companies, and technology firms, the project links protein engineering, biochemistry, analytical science and therapy development to speed up innovation. Based on real-life clinical case studies, NextBio focuses on blood clotting disorders and neurodegenerative diseases, helping identify which protein forms can be produced or targeted to create safer, more effective treatments.
Developing a Natural Decontamination Strategy for Hospital Drainage Channels (NaDeCon) – dr. Willem J.B. van Wamel (Erasmus MC)
Co-applicants: dr. Y.H. Choi (UL), prof. dr. N. Hermans (UAntwerpen), dr. B. Schrammeijer (HR), dr. J.A. Severin (EMC), dr. E. Tuenter (UAntwerpen)
Co-funders: Coöperatie Inno Growers U.A., VARTA Valorisatielab B.V.
Collaboration partners: Greenport West-Holland, Samenwerking Infectieziekten, Decorum, Biotrack
Antibiotic‑resistant bacteria such as Pseudomonas aeruginosa are regularly found in drains of healthcare facilities. The problem is that living bacterial cells can spread through hospital rooms via droplets, posing an acute threat to vulnerable patients. Because some of the bacteria in drains exist in tightly bound clusters (“biofilms”), current decontamination strategies prove insufficiently effective and are highly damaging to the environment. Plants produce substances with antimicrobial properties. However, these compounds have not yet been sufficiently studied for their applicability in sustainable infection prevention. This project addresses this societal challenge by developing natural, plant‑based solutions aimed at reducing biofilms in drains.
About NWO-KIC: innovation programmes in the knowledge and innovation covenant
NWO develops innovation programmes that focus on societal challenges in the Netherlands. These programmes aim to have an impact on the economy, people and society. The focus is on cooperation between knowledge institutions, private parties and the government. The results thus contribute to the realisation of economic opportunities. It is therefore essential that companies invest in every research project.
