Postgraduate Application FAQs
Applying to Warwick
Applying to Warwick
How to apply
Taught Research Teacher Training (PGCE)
Taught
Applications for most courses starting in September and October 2026 are now open.
The information below explains how to apply for most of our taught postgraduate courses. Some courses have different application processes. Find out more about applying for the Warwick MBA Link opens in a new window , MBChB Graduate Entry Medicine Link opens in a new window , and PGCE courses Link opens in a new window .
Stage 1: Prepare your application
Step 1: Choose a course Step 2: Experience Warwick Step 3: Check the entry requirements Step 4: Check the application key dates and deadlines Step 5: Explore your funding options
Step 1: Choose a course
Browse our courses Link opens in a new window .
You can apply for as many postgraduate courses as you want, but if more than one application is successful you can only accept a place on one full-time course.
Step 2: Experience Warwick
We understand how important it is to visit and explore your future university before you apply. That's why we have put together a range of online and in-person options to help you discover more about your course, visit campus, and get a sense of postgraduate life at Warwick.
Explore our postgraduate events Link opens in a new window and find out why Warwick could be the right choice for you Link opens in a new window .
Step 3: Check the entry requirements
Course-specific requirements
All courses have their own entry requirements, which can be different depending on the programme of study. Check the entry requirements on a case-by-case basis by searching for a course Link opens in a new window and visiting the course page.
Minimum academic qualifications
As a guide, the minimum standards we generally require for taught courses are at least a Lower Second Class UK Honours degree or equivalent, although some courses require an Upper Second Class or First Class Honours degree.
International students
International qualifications
We welcome applications from candidates offering many different international qualifications. You can see how your current degree score or GPA equates to the British system on the International Office equivalent scores page Link opens in a new window .
Supporting documents
When applying, please include your mark sheets/transcripts and the grading criteria used at your institution.
English language requirements
We require evidence that your English Language capability is of a level that will enable you to succeed on your chosen course of postgraduate study. Different courses ask for different standards of English language achievement which you can find on the course webpages. See further details on English language requirements .
Pre-sessional English courses
We also offer pre-sessional English courses Link opens in a new window that may help you to meet our English language requirements.
Step 4: Check the application key dates and deadlines
Applications for most courses starting in September and October 2026 are now open.
The on-time deadline for taught postgraduate courses starting in September/October 2026 is 2 August 2026. We will still accept late applications, however cannot guarantee that the application will be processed in time to complete the admissions process if received in September or there are a number of conditions to meet.
If applying late please include your degree certificates and transcripts, English proficiency evidence (if needed) and ensure referees can submit references promptly to allow time for CAS and VISA processing (if applicable). Some courses in Science and Engineering will also require you to apply for ATAS clearance..
If you have any queries, please contact our Postgraduate Admissions Service Link opens in a new window .
Step 5: Explore your funding options
There are a number of funding routes available Link opens in a new window to postgraduate students including postgraduate loans (Home and EU only), University scholarships, fee awards, and department bursaries and scholarships.
Stage 2: Ready to submit your application?
Step 1: Start your application Step 2: Application assessment fee Step 3: Supporting documents and academic references
Step 1: Start your application
Applicants for taught courses can apply online through our application portal. You don’t need to complete your application all at once, you can start now and save for later.
In order to avoid delays in receiving a decision, you'll need to upload scanned copies of all relevant supporting documents before you submit your application. Further information as to which supporting documents we require can be found below. You may find it helpful to save these in one place before you start your application.
Step 2: Application assessment fee
Before you can submit your application to us, you will be required to pay an application assessment fee of £75 (2025 entry). We aim to provide applicants with a decision as soon as possible after their application. We therefore begin processing applications immediately upon receipt of the application assessment fee. For this reason the application assessment fee is non-refundable. Other cancellation rights that applicants have are not affected.
Find more information about the application assessment fee and how to make a payment Link opens in a new window .
Step 3: Supporting documents and academic references
Once you start your application, the online system will inform you as to which supporting documents and information we require. The following information should help you prepare for your application.
All applicants
Degree certificate/transcripts from your university-level study (current or completed) including translations if not in English, grading scale and average score certificate if available.
Personal statement - see our guide to writing your personal statement .
Academic referee contact details - please refer to our  guide for providing academic references .
CV/resume (if applicable).
Certificates and transcripts should be uploaded in colour wherever possible.
Research
Applications for courses starting in academic years 2025/26 and 2026/27 are now open.
The information below explains how to apply for most of our research postgraduate courses. Some courses have different application processes. Find out more about applying for the Doctor of Business Administration Link opens in a new window and the Economics Master of Research (MRes/PhD) Link opens in a new window degrees.
If you’re a visiting postgraduate research student , please refer to our guidance for visiting students for full details, including course selection, dates, and required documentation.
Stage 1: Prepare your application
Step 1: Identify your research area of interest Step 2: Make contact with the academic department Step 3: Contact a supervisor Step 4: Draft your research proposal (if required by the department) Step 5: Check the entry requirements Step 6: Check the application key dates and deadlines Step 7: Explore your funding options
Step 1: Identify your research area of interest
Browse our list of research courses by department Link opens in a new window . Read around your chosen subject area as much as possible and start to draft your research idea.
You may want to consider whether our departments offer an advertised project or explore PhDs at jobs.ac.uk Link opens in a new window . You can use our search tool to explore the research areas of expertise in each of our departments.
Please visit PhD by Published Works Link opens in a new window for more information about an alternative PhD route that may be more suitable for you.
Step 2: Make contact with the academic department
For research degrees, one of the single most important points to consider is whether the academic department you are applying to shares your academic interests. All departments at Warwick are more than happy to discuss their areas of expertise with potential students, so we urge you to make contact before proceeding with your application any further.
Step 3: Contact a supervisor
Find a supervisor Link opens in a new window whose research aligns with your chosen subject area. We advise you to make contact with and secure a supervisor before you apply. If you cannot find a suitable potential supervisor, we would recommend that you send a short summary of your research interest to the administrator in your chosen department.
Please note that for applications to Warwick Business School (WBS), this step should be omitted. Find out more about how to apply for WBS courses Link opens in a new window .
Step 4: Draft your research proposal (if required by the department)
Different academic departments request different things as far as a proposal and/or application is concerned. You can find out more about research proposals by department Link opens in a new window on the course webpages or using our search tool , and we encourage you to discuss this with your department and/or potential supervisor directly.
Step 5: Check the entry requirements
Course-specific requirements
All courses have their own entry requirements, which can be different depending on the programme of study. Check the entry requirements on a case-by-case basis by searching for a course Link opens in a new window and visiting the course page.
Minimum academic qualifications
As a guide, the minimum standards we generally require for research courses are at least an Upper Second Class UK Honours degree or equivalent.
International students
International qualifications
We welcome applications from candidates offering many different international qualifications. You can see how your current degree score or GPA equates to the British system on the International Office equivalent scores table Link opens in a new window .
English language requirements
We require evidence that your English Language capability is of a level that will enable you to succeed on your chosen course of postgraduate study. Different courses ask for different standards of English language achievement which you can find on the course webpages. See further details on English language requirements .
Pre-sessional English courses
We also offer pre-sessional English courses Link opens in a new window that may help you to meet our English language requirements.
Step 6: Check the application key dates and deadlines
Applications for courses starting in academic years 2025/26 and 2026/27 are now open.
Some departments will publish application deadlines on their department website and/or course pages. Please check these pages carefully before you make your application. Places are often limited, so we recommend that you submit your application as early as possible.
Applicants requiring a visa to study
Some applicants may need to obtain a student visa to study in the UK.
For research courses that start in September and October 2026, the application deadline for students who require a visa to study in the UK is 2 August 2026.
For courses starting at other times of year we recommend that you submit your application a minimum of 3 months prior to your intended start date.
This should allow sufficient time to complete the admissions process and to obtain a visa to study in the UK.
We will still accept late applications, however cannot guarantee that the application will be processed in time to complete the admissions process if received late or there are a number of conditions to meet.
If applying late please include your degree certificates and transcripts, English proficiency evidence (if needed) and ensure referees can submit references promptly to allow time for CAS and visa processing (if applicable).
If you have any queries, please  contact our Postgraduate Admissions Service Link opens in a new window .

Step 1: Start your application
Apply for a research course online now through our application portal. You don’t need to complete your application all at once, you can start now and save for later.
In order to avoid delays in receiving a decision, you'll need to upload scanned copies of all relevant supporting documents before you submit your application. Further information as to which supporting documents we require can be found below. You may find it helpful to save these in one place before you start your application.
Step 2: Supporting documents and academic references

Personal statement - see our guide to writing your personal statement .

Academic referee contact details - please refer to our  guide for providing academic references .
CV/resume (if applicable).
