Funding Your Studies
Funding your studies
Studying in Finland is an investment in your future. Finland offers flexible study paths, a stable society and equal opportunities, but careful financial planning is an essential part of preparing for your studies.
Share
Key facts
Plan to cover your tuition fees and living costs independently.
Students from outside the EU/EEA and Switzerland usually pay tuition for English-taught bachelor’s and master’s programmes.
Tuition fees are typically €8,000–€20,000 per year, depending on the programme.
University scholarships are competitive and usually cover tuition fees only, not living costs.
Doctoral programmes do not charge tuition fees, but doctoral students must fund their living costs.
Living costs are approximately €900–€1,200 per month.
Part-time work is allowed, but challenging to find - it should not be your main funding plan.
Getting started
Planning a realistic budget for your time in Finland is an important part of preparing for the journey ahead.
You should be prepared to cover all your living costs independently. Students from outside the EU/EEA or Switzerland are also generally required to pay tuition fees for English-taught bachelor’s and master’s programmes.
Although students with a student residence permit may work up to 30 hours per week on average, you should not rely on part-time work to cover your living costs or tuition fees.
Moreover, finding part-time work is challenging - especially without local language skills! Remember that your studies should remain your main priority.
Some universities may offer scholarships or tuition fee waivers both for bachelor's and master's level international students. Funding options also exist for doctoral level.

Fees and Cost of Living
Fees and Cost of Living
Quality education is a valuable investment. Prepare to finance your studies by paying the tuition fees and covering your everyday living costs. As they say, good things are worth the price!
Share
Tuition fees
Students from outside the EU/EEA and Switzerland usually need to pay tuition fees for English-taught bachelor’s and master’s programmes. Some students may be exempt based on their residence status or other grounds, so always check the current rules and your own situation.
Fees vary by university and programme, ranging from €8,000 to €20,000 per year. Check the exact tuition fee, payment arrangements and possible refund conditions on on Studyinfo.fi or with the university you are applying to.
Applicants from outside the EU/EEA or Switzerland also need to pay the EUR 100 application fee .
Degree programmes taught in Finnish or Swedish do not charge tuition fees. Doctoral programmes are also tuition-free regardless of nationality.
If you are paying tuition, you are often also eligible to apply for scholarshipsthat may be offered by the university .
Living expenses
Beyond tuition, you need to be able to cover your living costs independently.
Finland is a relatively expensive country to live in, so while the official minimum requirement set by Finnish Immigration Service Migri is €800 per month (€9,600 per year), we recommend you reserve a bit more money for your living expenses, approximately €900–€1200 per month, depending on the location. This includes food, accommodation , and travel.
You must prove sufficient finances and insurance when applying for a student residence permit .
Student union fees
University students pay an annual student union fee of about €50–€70 per academic year, granting access to discounts in student restaurants and public transport. Student union membership at universities of applied sciences (UAS) is optional.
Your Finnish university can provide advice on student union and healthcare fees, among other practicalities.
Student healthcare fee
If you are studying for a bachelor's or a master's degree: in order to use the services of the Finnish Student Health Service FSHS , you'll be required to pay an annual student healthcare fee - currently around 35 euros per term (that's about 70 euros per year).
Can I work part-time during studies?
• As an international student you are allowed to work part-time up to 30 hours per week on average during the academic year and full-time during holidays.
• Finding employment during your studies is challenging, especially without Finnish/Swedish skills. What&#039;s more, possible part-time job salaries would not be sufficient to cover both tuition and living costs.
• You should not base your financial plans on the uncertain prospect of finding part-time employment during your studies.
• Focus on skills, internships, volunteering and networking for long-term success!

Bachelor&#039;s and Master&#039;s Scholarships
Bachelor&#039;s and Master&#039;s Scholarships
Scholarships can help you manage tuition costs in Finland, but they are competitive and do not cover any living costs. Understanding what scholarship options exist will help you plan wisely.
Share
How to apply for a scholarship?
Scholarships and tuition fee waivers in Finland are offered by the individual universities - there are no governmental scholarships available .
If the university of your choice offers scholarships, remember that they are competitive and will typically only cover your tuition fees, either partially or wholly. You must independently cover your own living costs .
With some universities, you can apply for a scholarship at the same time as you submit your admission application — making it easy to plan your studies and funding together. In other cases, scholarships may become available after you’ve started your studies. These can for example reward your progress, academic success, or even your growing Finnish language skills.
Find your programme
Start by picking the degree programme that interests you the most. You can find a suitable programme either on the universities' own websites or at Studyinfo.fi .
Explore scholarships
Once you have found a suitable study programme, check if the programme offers scholarships, tuition fee waivers or "early bird" tuition fee options for non-EU/EEA students. You can typically find instructions for applying for a scholarship alongside the application information.
If you have questions or need a bit more guidance, check out the scholarship information on the universities' own websites .
Scholarships for studying in Finland - facts
You may have seen posts and ads promoting “Fully funded Finland government scholarships” that would cover everything from university tuition fees to travel and living costs in Finland.
Be cautious - those claims are misleading. Posts and ads like that contain a lot of false information.

Doctoral Funding
Explore various doctoral (PhD) funding opportunities and tap into diverse sources ready to support your academic journey and ambitions.
Share
Navigating doctoral (PhD) funding options
In Finland, there are no tuition fees for doctoral (PhD) students, but you must finance your own living expenses. Start at university departments for funding guidance. They provide insights into paid doctoral positions and scholarships that align closely with your field of study.
Resources
Check out the Research Council of Finland and Research.fi for research funding possibilities. Paid doctoral positions may also be available - you can search for them for example via the Euraxess.fi platform, or on the universities' websites .
For further information, contact your Finnish university department.
EDUFI Fellowships - programme discontinued in October 2025
The EDUFI Fellowships programme for doctoral students and researchers has been discontinued, so applications to that programme are no longer considered. See the notification on EDUFI main pages: The EDUFI Fellowships programme has ended in 2025 .

FAQ
FAQ
Got questions about studying in Finland? Here you’ll find answers to some of the most common questions international students ask about applying, tuition fees and scholarships, living in Finland, residence permits, working while studying and life after graduation.
Share
Getting started
University or University of Applied Sciences - what is the difference?
Finland has two types of higher education institutions: universities and universities of applied sciences (UAS).
Universities focus on academic learning and scientific research, while UAS institutions take a more practical, career-oriented approach with strong links to working life. Universities offer bachelor’s, master’s and doctoral degrees, while UAS institutions offer bachelor’s and master’s degrees.
Your choice depends on your goals: a university may suit you if you are interested in research and academic study, while a UAS may be a better fit if you prefer practical, professionally oriented learning.
What kind of degree programmes can I study in English?
Finland offers around 600 English-taught bachelor’s and master’s degree programmes in a wide range of fields. You can find English-taught programmes and their admission details on Studyinfo.fi and on the universities’ own websites . Some fields and degree programmes like Medicine for example are available only in Finnish or Swedish.
What are the admission requirements?
Admission requirements vary by degree programme. For a bachelor’s degree, you generally need an upper secondary qualification that gives you eligibility for higher education. For a master’s degree, you need a relevant bachelor’s degree, and master’s programmes at universities of applied sciences (UAS) also require relevant work experience. Some programmes may have additional requirements, such as entrance examinations or interviews. Always check the exact eligibility criteria and required documents on Studyinfo.fi and with the university you are applying to.
Do I need IELTS or TOEFL?
It depends on the degree programme. If you need to prove your English proficiency, commonly accepted tests include IELTS, TOEFL, PTE and Cambridge English qualifications, but some applicants may qualify through other evidence or exemptions. Always check the exact language requirements for your chosen programme on Studyinfo.fi and the university’s website.
How do I apply to study in Finland?
You apply to Finnish universities and universities of applied sciences (UAS) online through Studyinfo.fi , Finland’s official national application portal. First find the degree programme you want to apply to, then check its application period, admission requirements and required documents. Some programmes use the national joint application, while others have separate application periods.
When can I apply to study in Finland?
Application periods depend on the degree programme. The main joint application for English-taught bachelor’s and master’s programmes starting in autumn is usually held in January. A smaller number of programmes starting in January may be available in an autumn joint application. Some programmes use separate or rolling applications with different schedules. Always check the exact application period for your chosen programme on Studyinfo.fi or with the university you are planning to apply to .
Fees and funding
Do I need to pay tuition fees in Finland? Or can I study for free?
Students from outside the EU/EEA and Switzerland generally need to pay tuition fees for English-taught bachelor’s and master’s degree programmes in Finland. Tuition fees typically range from €8,000 to €20,000 per year, depending on the university and programme.
EU/EEA and Swiss students generally do not pay tuition fees. Doctoral programmes do not charge tuition fees, regardless of nationality. Exchange students also generally do not pay tuition fees to the Finnish host university.
Are scholarships available in Finland?
Yes. Finnish universities and universities of applied sciences may offer scholarships or tuition fee waivers to students who are required to pay tuition fees. Scholarship schemes, eligibility criteria and amounts vary by university, and they usually cover tuition fees partly or fully rather than living costs. There are no Finnish government scholarships available. Check the scholarship options directly with the university you are applying to, and plan to cover your living expenses independently.
Living in Finland
What is student life like in Finland?
Student life in Finland combines independent study with active student communities and a wide range of student services. Universities and student organisations offer activities, clubs and opportunities to meet other students, while student discounts can help with everyday costs such as meals and transport. Campus life varies by university and city, so explore the university and destination you are interested in to get a better picture of daily student life.
How much does it cost to live in Finland?
We recommend budgeting approximately €900–€1,200 per month for living expenses in Finland. Your actual costs will depend on where you live and your personal spending, with accommodation typically taking the largest share of your budget. If you are required to pay tuition fees, remember to budget for these separately. You should plan your finances so that you can cover all your living costs independently throughout your studies.
Can I work while studying in Finland?
If you have a Finnish residence permit for studies, you are allowed to work in paid employment for an average of up to 30 hours per week. You may work more hours during some weeks or holidays as long as your average working time remains within the permitted limit. Work that forms part of your degree, such as certain internships or thesis work, may be exempt from the limit. Check the current rules on the Finnish Immigration Service (Migri) website .
Finding part-time work can be challenging, particularly without Finnish or Swedish skills, so you should not rely on employment to finance your studies.
Can I get by in English?
Yes. You can complete an English-taught degree programme in English, and English is widely used in many everyday situations in Finland. However, learning Finnish or Swedish can make everyday life easier and significantly broaden your opportunities for internships, part-time work and employment after graduation. We recommend starting to learn one of Finland’s national languages while you study.
Can I bring my family with me?
Eligible family members of a student can apply for a residence permit to join them in Finland. Your family must meet the Finnish Immigration Service’s financial requirements, which depend on the size of your family and where in Finland you live. A family member who receives a residence permit on the basis of family ties has an unrestricted right to work and study in Finland, however student family housing is limited and employment can be difficult without local-language skills. Check the eligibility criteria, financial requirements and application instructions on the Finnish Immigration Service (Migri) website before making plans.
Practical things
Do I need a residence permit to study in Finland?
If you are coming to Finland from outside the EU/EEA to complete a degree, you will generally need a residence permit for studies. The application requires documentation including proof of your study place, sufficient funds and valid insurance. Residence rules differ for EU/EEA citizens and for shorter stays. Always check the current requirements with the Finnish Immigration Service (Migri) .
How can I avoid fake universities or misleading information?
Use official sources when checking Finnish universities, degree programmes, admissions and scholarships. Studyinfinland.fi is Finland’s official guide for international students, while Studyinfo.fi is the official national service for finding degree programmes and applying to higher education. You should also verify information on the university’s own official website. Be cautious of fake universities , as well as organisations or social-media accounts promising guaranteed admission, guaranteed visas or “fully funded Finnish government scholarships”.
After graduation
Can I stay in Finland after I graduate?
Yes. If you complete a degree in Finland and do not yet have a job, you may be eligible to apply for a residence permit to look for work or start a business. The permit can be granted for a maximum of two years, subject to the Finnish Immigration Service’s eligibility and financial requirements. If you already have a job after graduating, a different residence permit based on having completed a degree in Finland may be appropriate. Check the current options and application requirements on the Finnish Immigration Service (Migri) website.
Other questions
Where can I find info about high school or vocational studies in Finland?
Study in Finland focuses on higher education at Finnish universities and universities of applied sciences. For information about general upper secondary education, vocational education and training, and other study options in Finland, visit Studyinfo.fi . InfoFinland.fi also provides practical information about education and living in Finland in several languages.
What is Study in Finland?
Study in Finland is the official guide to Finnish higher education for international students, maintained by the Finnish National Agency for Education. We provide information about studying and living in Finland, including degree programmes, applications, tuition fees and scholarships, student life and what to do after graduation.
For detailed admission requirements, always check Studyinfo.fi and the university you are applying to.
For residence permits and immigration matters, refer to the Finnish Immigration Service (Migri).
